## Description

These are follow-ups from the Air's review of SL-410.p1, both non-blocking.

**1. Statement stops misfire at the start of a `match`'s arm list.**
- **Example:** in `match x {`, then `1 => 2`, then `}`, the lane records `syntax.rule.assignment-target` at the `=` (3:11). sawc2 says "expected `case` to begin an arm" at 3:9, which is the grammar's actual reason: an arm starts with `case`.
- **Four more stops misfire at the same spot:**
  - `a b` → juxtaposition;
  - `a.. == c` → range-open-end;
  - `borrow var e =` → borrow-form;
  - `1 = 2` → assignment-target.
- **The head doesn't matter:** `match 1 {`, `match (x) {`, a call head, a member head and a value `match` all misfire.
- **The mechanism:** `Stops._ended` checks only that the statement ending at the stop has a tree of its own. Here that statement sits in a `block-body` that the chart can read only as a trailing closure on the head, and the trailing-closure and head-restriction filters refuse that reading. The stops don't check the filters.
- **Not affected:** struct, enum, trait, extension, import and parameter lists, where no closure reading exists (12 texts checked).
- **Fix:** `_ended`, and `_assignment_target`'s held list, require that the holder's own reading survives the filters.
- **Pin:** `match x {` / `1 => 2` / `}` records what sawc2 says, the plain arm refusal (or whatever the rules then give; state it). Add one case per stop at that spot.
- **Why it matters:** `1 => 2` is the natural Rust-style mistake.
- **Probes:** the Air's `.build/scratch/air-review/sl410/cases_arms*/` and `cases_stops*/`.

**2. The ascii-identifier hint** always ends "an interpolation's expression is source". That clause should appear only when the character is in an interpolation segment.

SL-446.p1 is up: the two follow-ups from SL-410's review. It has 2 commits and touches 11 files (+398/−35).

**1. A stop reads only the readings the rules leave.**
- **The mechanism was wider than the description said.** Every stop read every Earley item at its token, including items from readings the filters had already refused.
  - range-open-end and borrow-form fired straight from such items;
  - statement-separator fired from a `;` completion, and move-place from a `move-expr` completion;
  - the token-only stops, float-point and shift-adjacent, fired even where no surviving reading reached the token.
- **A sibling:** the same misfire at statement level inside `1 {` or `(x) {`, whose only statement-holding reading is a trailing closure on a literal or a parenthesized expression. sawc2 says trailing-closure there.
- **Not affected:** struct, enum and the other lists with no closure reading.
- **The fix,** in `recognize.py`:
  - `Stops._survives` walks an item up its holders to the start symbol, building partial derivations. An item survives if some path has every complete construct with a tree, and no `PREFIX_FILTERS` rule (`_trailing_closure`, `_head_restriction`) refusing a partly read construct.
  - Every stop reads through it, including `_ended`, `_held` and `_awaits`, and statement-separator and move-place now use `_ended`.
  - `refusals()` names nothing when no surviving item reaches the stop token.
  - The walk memoizes above a head reset.
- **Why it's sound:** both prefix filters decide a trailing closure at its `{`, by its callee or the head it stands in. Nothing after the `{` changes that, so every completion of a refused partial reading is refused too.
  - `_closure_start` returns None for a hop with no closure yet: a closure not begun refuses nothing.
  - `_generic_or_less` was tried and left out, since no partial reading needs it (`prepare` commits a list before `(`, `.` or `{`).
- **Pins:**
  - 18 cases in `negative/control.saw` (`syntax.expr.match-arm/…`) all record `parse-error`. They cover `1 => 2` and one case per stop where an arm begins (`1 = 2`, `a b`, `a.. == c`, `borrow var e =`, `;`, `a;`, `move x.f()`, `7.`, `a < < b`), across literal, parenthesized, call, member, generic, comparison and match-value scrutinees, and inside a trailing closure.
  - Two trailing-closure siblings in `negative/closures.saw`.
  - `test_recognize.py`'s `PARTIAL_READINGS`: removing either prefix filter brings assignment-target back.
- **Controls keep their names:** juxtaposition in an `if`/`while` body, a trailing closure on a name and an arm's block body; assignment-target in an arm body and at statement level.
- **Nothing else moved:** `cases.py --check`, `negative.py`'s rewrite and `corpus.py --write` are all unchanged.
- The README Refusals section gains a paragraph.

**2. The ascii-identifier hint's clause** ("an interpolation's expression is source") now appears only in a segment.
- `Lexer.segment`, with `lex_segment(source:)` and `lex` both through `lex_as`.
- `parse_segment` calls `lex_segment`: 2 lines in `parser.saw`.
- The golden `error_non_ascii_character.tokens` loses the clause, and `compiler/lex/tests/errors.saw` pins both hints.
