## Description

**`var _` is accepted in patterns, though `_` binds nothing there is to mutate.** Found by SL-978's census (rows c24, c27, c29): `case Some(var _)`, `case Two(var _, var _)`, `if var _ = opt` and `while var _ = …` all compile and behave like `_`. Only the statement `var _ = e` is refused (`syntax.stmt.refused-var-discard`).

**Ruled (user, Nov 5): `var _` is a bug at every position.** Nothing can be mutated through it, and in a `match` on a borrowed place a `var` binding may take an exclusive borrow, so `var _` could raise an exclusivity error for a name that doesn't exist.

**Fix:**
- Refuse `var _` at every position a `var` binding can stand: payload patterns, `if var`, `while var`, and any other the census finds. Use the statement form's rule and reason, with a fix-it to `_` (in a payload) or `let` (`if let _`, `while let _`).
- **One funnel (obligation 1):** a single parser check that every `var`-binding position goes through, with its entry points named in its docstring.
- GRAMMAR.md: extend `syntax.stmt.refused-var-discard`, or add a sibling rule, to cover the pattern positions. Update the spec's "Discard binding" text to match.
- Sweep std, `compiler/`, `blade/`, `libs/` and `tests/corpus/` for `var _`, rewriting each use to `_` or `let _`. Record any corpus verdict move.
- **Pins:** a refusal fixture per position. SL-978's probe `wildcard_var_payloads.saw` becomes a refusal, since that's its point, and is rewritten or removed.

**SL-980.p1: `var _` is refused at every `var`-binding position** (user ruling, Nov 5)

- **Before:** only the statement `var _ = e` was refused, by `parse_let`. Eleven other positions accepted it:
  - payloads `case Some(var _)` and `case Some(borrow var _)`;
  - `if`, `else if`, `while` and `guard var _`, including over a lend;
  - `if` and `while borrow var _`;
  - a `borrow` block's first and later bindings.

  `for var _` and `for borrow var _` reached `slice.not-yet`.
- **Now:** one parser funnel, `refused_var_discard`, refuses all of them under `syntax.rule.discard-binding`, at the `var`, with "`_` binds nothing, so there is nothing to mutate". Its docstring lists nine entry points. The fix-it depends on the position:
  - a payload: `_`;
  - `if`, `while`, `guard` and the `borrow` heads: `let _`;
  - a `borrow` block: `borrow let _`, or `let _` for a later binding;
  - `for`: `for _ in`.

  Every suggested spelling compiles and runs. The statement keeps its own `syntax.stmt.refused-var-discard`.
- **Grammar:** the recognizer's `_discard_binding` filter covers every `var`-binding alternative (`VAR_BINDINGS`, nine `FILTERS` rows). GRAMMAR.md's rule row lists all eleven productions, and the spec's "Discard binding" covers every position with a pinned `saw-error` example. `CLAIMS.tsv` and the generated `grammar.saw` don't change.
- **Pins:** 14 negative parse cases (`negative/declarations.saw`, written by `cases.py --write`). The stale waiver line is removed.
- **Sweep:** no corpus, std, blade or libs file used `var _` in a pattern.
  - SL-978's `wildcard_var_payloads.saw` now uses `Some(_)` / `Two(_, _)`, keeping its point that several wildcards bind nothing.
  - The resolve fixture `name.undefined.wildcard-payload.saw` reads `_` after `Some(_)`, and is still `name.undefined`.
- **Gates:**
  - `run.py --only seed,units,golden,subset,fixture_whitespace,std,grammar_lint,recognizer,grammar_parse,parser,resolver,programs`: ok;
  - docverify: ok;
  - the full grammar corpus: ok, 10,159 files;
  - no corpusdiff move.

**Open, small:** a `borrow var _ = v[0] { … }` block at the start of a statement reads two ways, both refused: this rule, and `syntax.rule.borrow-form`'s place-assignment reading. sawc reports discard-binding. Pinning the statement form needs a precedence line in GRAMMAR.md. The value form, `let x = borrow var _ = v[0] { 1 }`, is pinned.
