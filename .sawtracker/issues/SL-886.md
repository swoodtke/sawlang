**The std.json section matches `std/json.saw`:**
- the seven cases and their labels;
- `@synthesize` ExplicitCopy;
- `parse(text:max_depth:max_size:)` with defaults 64 and 16_777_216, returning `Result<JsonValue, DecodeError>`;
- `to_json_string() -> Result<String, EncodeError>`;
- the five optional readers;
- `as_array`/`as_object` as `&var self` `borrows -> &var …?`;
- the four error and fault types with `describe`/`format`.

**Its behavioral claims hold on main** (`j1_json_claims.saw`, ASan):
- **Malformed inputs:** `[1,`, `{"a" 1}`, `tru`, a lone `\uD800`, `01` and `[1] x` each give an `Err` naming a byte offset, never a panic.
- **Depth:** 64 levels parse; 65 is "nesting is deeper than the decoder allows" at byte 64.
- **Non-finite floats:** `NaN` gives `Err` ("no representation in this format").
- **Integers:** `9223372036854775807` is a `Number`; `9223372036854775808` is a `Float`.

**One wording nit (non-blocking):** "a number token that is integral and fits `Int` is a `Number`". `1.0` and `1e3` are integral VALUES, but both parse as `Float`. The rule is lexical: a token with no fraction and no exponent, which fits `Int`. Consider "a number token with no fraction or exponent that fits `Int`".
