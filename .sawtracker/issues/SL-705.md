**sawc2 holds one retain too many inside a destructuring `let` of a borrowed projection (V112).** Exposed by SL-701 (the `Arc` port), which made the program build.

`examples/conformance/V112_destructuring_let_retains_a_projection.saw` (and its `tests/corpus` twin), at line 25 of its output:
- sawc2: `E4 if-let fresh        inside 5 after 3`
- Stage 0 and the file's expectation: `... inside 4 after 3`

The count inside is one higher under sawc2; after the construct both agree (3). So sawc2 takes an extra retain for the destructured projection and releases it at the end. Whether that's a duplicated retain or a missing release-at-bind isn't known yet.

**Why it matters:** V112 is a conformance row: "a destructuring let retains a projection" exactly once. A count that differs is an ownership-accounting divergence in the transfer path, the kind gmgate exists to catch. That it nets out afterwards suggests a misplaced pair, not a leak, but it must be reduced to the statement that takes the extra retain.

**To do:** reduce to the destructuring form (`E4`, an `if let` over a fresh value) and compare sawc2's MIR (`sawc2 mir`) with the drops lane's record; name the mechanism (obligation 4) and sweep the sibling forms (`let (a, _) = …`, `match`, `while let`) that V112's other cases cover.

LANGUAGE_SPEC.md:7276-7279: "A temporary ... is destroyed at the end of its statement ... a condition of `if`, `while` or `guard`, and a `match` guard, ends before the branch it decides."

- **Note:** The statement is `if let (a, _) = fresh_pair(&base) { e4 = base.strong_count() }`: the `_` leaf of a fresh scrutinee is still held while the branch body runs. The count nets out after the branch (3), so no leak. Priority: low-normal (a conformance row fails).
