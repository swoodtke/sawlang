## Description

**About 35 run-lane probes and corpus programs order their output by timers, or pass only within a measured time margin, so load can change their verdict.** Found by SL-975's sweep (`.build/scratch/p975/`: `stall_sweep1.log`, `probe_stall.py`, `stall_list.txt`). SL-975 itself fixes K90 and the deadline-poll and sleep-then-read programs. These need a redesign.

**Groups** (frozen-run evidence: runs stopped and resumed at random moments, 16 tries each):
- **Order set by timers:**
  - `taskgroup_sleep_ordering` (5 of 16 failed frozen);
  - 24 of the 31 `join_inversion_*` probes (1–5 of 16);
  - the ten `running_task_dump_*_drive` probes (a 300 ms hold).

  When two timers expire together, slots resume in slot order, not deadline order.
- **Measured margins in the passing path:**
  - `offload_cancelled_during/before_the_call_parks` and `pool_teardown_drives_while_a_worker_ends` (at least 10 ticks in 200 ms; 10–11 of 16 failed);
  - `background_close_parks_over_an_offload` (14 of 16);
  - `offload_parked_task_defers_the_deadlock_report` (3 of 16);
  - `process_output_cancel_ends_its_drain` (4 of 16) and `process_output_cancel_reaps_child` (2 of 16);
  - `thread_receive_both_contexts`, `thread_join_both_contexts` and `pool_waits_in_a_task_run_siblings`;
  - `thread_receive_gate_allocation_refused`, `background_close_waits_on_a_join` and `offload_seam_first_tick`;
  - `process_run_concurrent`, `process_output_concurrent` and `process_cancel_during_child`;
  - the `quick<1000` checks in `borrows_body_suspends_*`, `consumes_suspending_cancel_and_unjoined` and `init_suspends`;
  - `process_output_waits_for_no_other_child`, `taskgroup_cancel_during_sleep`, `task_backtrace_panic/churn/nest` and `prelude_import_makes_visible`.

**Direction:** each program states what it pins without depending on the machine's speed.
- **Order:** set it with explicit gates (a channel or atomic the earlier step signals), not timer lengths.
- **"Something ran while X waited":** prove it by an event the sibling causes, not a tick count in a time window.
- **Waits:** use TESTING.md rule 3 (a generous deadline, then a panic).

Some offloaded calls can only end on a condition through a C helper in `probes/`, for example a blocking `read` on a pipe the sibling writes. Whether to allow one is a question for the user. Pin each fixed program as SL-975 does: 200 runs at a 1-minute load above 20, every run giving its stated output.

**1. Expired sleepers resume in slot order, and the spec says earliest-deadline.**
- **The probe** (`expired_order.saw`, main's compiler and std):
  - spawn the 30 ms sleeper first (slot 0), then the 10 ms one;
  - `yield_now()`, so both register their sleeps;
  - block the executor's thread for 100 ms in a `sync` helper, so both deadlines pass;
  - then join.
- **The result:** the 30 ms sleeper prints first, 5 of 5 runs. Joining the 10 ms task first (`expired_order_b.saw`) changes nothing, 5 of 5.
- **Why that matters:** LANGUAGE_SPEC's scheduler paragraph says "`sleep(d)` is scheduled earliest-deadline over relative sleeps". This is the case the old test hit under load.
- **The rewrite keeps the test from reaching that case:** the timers are now 10 ms against 20 s, and the new header says a stall "would leave slot order, not deadline order, to pick who runs first". So the program now documents, as expected behaviour, something the spec seems to rule out.
- **Which is right needs a ruling.** Either the executor should resume expired sleepers in deadline order, and the old shape plus a forced stall becomes the pin; or the spec sentence should say the order among deadlines that have already passed is unspecified.

Approved patch SL-977.p2 (revision 1)

**Air: SL-977.p2 (unit 2), approved.**

**Read:**
- **The offload probes.** `poll`'s `pollfd` points at a local of the suspending caller, which lives in its frame, so the offload's pointer rule holds. `PollFd` matches `struct pollfd`. `main` can see `IN_CALL` only once the caller is parked in the offload, since nothing suspends between the store and the park. So the result proves both claims: the call returned on the sibling's byte, and the sibling ran while the cancelled caller waited.
- **The process probes.**
  - The gate sits at the program's own standard input, and its standard error points at the "alive" pipe only while a child launches. Children inherit just fds 0–2.
  - So closing the gate's write end releases every child. With the parent's own write end closed first, end of file on the alive pipe means every child has exited.
  - Each wait restores standard error before it panics.
- **K188's row** now describes the probe's pipe wait, which is accurate.

**Run on the Air:** the 9 probes at -O0 and -O2, 50 runs each, one process at a time. A run passes when it panics exactly as its header says and shows the stated lines in order. 900 runs, 0 failures. The compiler was main's at your base (89102f343).

**A note, not blocking:** a panic raised inside a launch window, between `divert_error()` and `restore_error()`, by something other than the waits (say a refused spawn's `try!` in a cell) writes its message into the alive pipe. The probe still fails by its exit status, but the reason is lost. If you touch these again, the cell could restore standard error on its own failure paths.
