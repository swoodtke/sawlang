## Description

Unit U2a of the self-hosted compiler epic (SL-398): the formal grammar. Its output is a shared document, SL:grammar, drafted locally and published by the lead after review. It touches no repository file, so it runs alongside U1 (SL-399) without conflict.

## Why

The parser must handle all valid Saw, and its tests must prove it. The plan (user, Sep 25: "that's exactly what i had in mind"):
1. **U2a (this unit):** a formal, machine-readable grammar of the whole language, with a stable name on every production and alternative, and the contexts each construct may appear in.
2. **U2b:** a generator walks that grammar and emits snippets paired with their expected canonical AST dumps. It adds golden cases for the known tricky points, a negative (mutation) corpus, and a coverage lane that fails when an alternative or a construct-in-context pair goes untested.
3. **U4 onward:** the parser, written test-first against U2b's corpus.

So this grammar is the single authority for syntax that the parser, the tests and the spec all cite. It must be complete, precise, and consumable by a program.

## Sources

The spec is the authority. Where the Python parser differs, the spec wins, and the difference is listed.
- `LANGUAGE_SPEC.md`, all of it, and the lockdown docs `SL:borrowing` and `SL:testing` (`~/bin/sawtracker doc show SL:borrowing`), which add and change syntax.
- The rulings that live outside the spec: the SL-393 list (`~/bin/sawtracker show SL-393`), which covers SL-59 statement arms, SL-73 the general postfix call, SL-309 `??` in cast targets (the spec is wrong there), SL-310 trailing closures, SL-45, design 274's owed items, and SL-380's per-hop postfix charging. Also `designs/259-selfhost-parser.md` (rulings R1–R8), `designs/274-parser-track-reconciliation.md` (N1–N12, B1, U5′) and `designs/reviews/parser-census-sep1.md`.
- The token set: the lexer in `selfhost/lexer/src/lib.saw`. U1 is moving it to `compiler/lex/`; read whichever exists.
- Evidence of what parses today: `sawc/parser/`. You may also write scratch probes under `.build/scratch/` and compile them with `./.venv/bin/python sawc/sawc.py` (see CLAUDE.md, "Scratch compilations"). Evidence only, never authority.
- The node vocabulary the prototypes used: `prototypes/minivm/M18_AST.md` (a reference, not a constraint).

## Output: `.build/scratch/docs/grammar.md`

1. **Notation.** A small, regular EBNF, defined at the top, that a Python generator can parse: productions, alternatives, optional, zero-or-more, one-or-more, terminals as token kinds or quoted spellings, and a way to attach a name to each alternative. Keep it boring and unambiguous.
2. **The lexical layer**, briefly:
   - the token kinds (from the lexer);
   - where a newline ends a statement, and where it is insignificant (inside `()`, `[]` and committed generic `<>`, design 129; trailing-operator continuation, R3);
   - interpolation;
   - doc-comment trivia;
   - contextual words.
3. **The grammar**, area by area, covering *everything* the spec and the lockdown docs define:
   - declarations: `func`, `struct`, `enum` (raw backings), `trait`, `extension`, `init`, `static`, `const`, `import` in every form, `extern` blocks, and the attributes (`@export`, `@section`, `@align`, `@synthesize`, and every `@test` form);
   - types: paths and qualifiers, generics and const generics, defaults, `T?`, `&T`/`&var T`, slices `&[T]`, `[T; N]`, tuples and named tuples, function types with effects (`unsafe`, `sync`, `borrows`, `borrows(sync)`, `consumes`), and `any Trait`;
   - expressions:
     - a precedence and associativity table;
     - literals, including interpolated strings, collection literals and repeat literals;
     - calls with labels, trailing closures, and the general postfix call;
     - closures and capture lists;
     - `move`, `try` / `try!` / `try?`, the `catch` forms, `??`, `?.`, `!`, `as` casts, ranges;
     - `if` and `match` as values;
     - the `borrow` statement and argument forms;
   - statements: `let`/`var` (with destructuring), assignment and compound assignment, `return`/`break`/`continue`, `for`/`while`/`loop`, `guard`, `if let`/`while let`, `match` with statement and expression arms, the `borrow` block, and `lend`;
   - patterns: literals, ranges, guards, tuples, enum payloads, bindings, and wildcards;
   - the lockdown forms: `borrow let|var` blocks, the statement and argument forms, `for borrow let|var` (Proposed), `func []` and `func []=`, multi-argument subscripts, the `default:` label, tuple lends, and `borrows(sync)`.
4. **A stable name on every production and every alternative:** `syntax.<area>.<construct>[.<variant>]`, lowercase, words joined by hyphens (for example `syntax.expr.call.trailing-closure`). Tests will cite these names (SL:testing §6). Each production also carries:
   - its spec reference (section heading);
   - its status: `current`, `lockdown` (new or changed by SL:borrowing or SL:testing), `retired` (still parsed but refused later with a hint, such as inline place writes), `removed` (a parse error, such as `#lend_var`), or `pending` (awaiting a ruling);
   - a preliminary AST node kind, which U2b turns into the dump contract.
5. **The context table.** For every expression-level and statement-level construct, the contexts it may appear in, and the restrictions in each. Contexts include:
   - statement, tail (block value), operand, argument, receiver, index;
   - interpolation, condition (`if`/`while`/`guard`), match scrutinee, match arm body, closure body;
   - initializer, default parameter value, static initializer, const-generic argument, return value.

   Restrictions include, for example, whether a trailing closure is allowed in a condition. This is the matrix the generator walks. Missed contexts are where most of the Python parser's bugs lived.
6. **Disambiguation rules.** Each known tricky point, with its ruled resolution and source:
   - generic `<` versus less-than;
   - a trailing closure versus a block;
   - newline significance;
   - `??` and `?` after `as T?`;
   - statement versus value tails;
   - `x = 1 y` (B1, refused);
   - a leading minus after a block;
   - struct-init versus call, which is gone: there is one `Call` node;
   - flat `else if`;
   - postfix chains charged per hop;
   - the 256 depth limit and exactly what counts against it.
7. **Open questions.** Everything the spec leaves unsaid or contradicts, each with the options. Mark the productions that depend on the three pending SL:borrowing rulings (threads t21 to t23; for example `if borrow var x = e` and `case Some(borrow var x)`) as `pending`, with the proposed spelling.
8. **The one-time check against today's parser.** For each area, note where `sawc/parser/` accepts or refuses something differently from your grammar, with a probe file where you ran one. These become U2b golden cases or open questions.

## Rules
- Read-only except for your output file and scratch probes under `.build/scratch/`. Do not touch `compiler/`, `selfhost/`, `tools/`, `build.sh` or any tracked file, since U1 is working there. No tracker writes.
- Do not run the test suite or any battery lane.
- Style: plain, direct sentences. The EBNF itself should be clean enough that a reader learns the language's syntax from it.
- Completeness beats polish. When unsure whether something is syntax, include it and mark it.

- the context-table size;
- the open questions;
- the differences from today's parser;
- anything the lead or the user must rule on.

Review of the brief (claude-air-sawlang), checked against LANGUAGE_SPEC and the lockdown docs. This grammar becomes the single authority for syntax, so gaps in the brief's inventory become gaps in the grammar. Six points:

1. **Two listed constructs do not exist.**
   - **`loop`.** The spec says "there is no `loop` keyword (it was dropped as redundant)", and the reservation is retired (design 55). The infinite loop is a conditionless `while { }`.
   - **`const` declarations.** Under "Compile-Time Evaluation", "`const func`, macros, and compile-time reflection below are still *planned*". The only current `const` is the const-generic parameter, `<const N: Int>`. If `const func` goes in, it should be marked `pending`.
2. **Declarations the list omits:**
   - `type` aliases (contextual `type`), at module level and as associated types in traits and extensions;
   - the inline `module m { … }`;
   - `borrows struct`, `unsafe struct`, and `unsafe static var`;
   - the visibility modifiers: `public`, `public(package)`, `public(parent)`, and the contextual `private` on fields;
   - `public import` re-exports;
   - `static func` methods (design 236), and `consumes` in a method's effect slot, which has a canonical order;
   - top-level `static_assert`;
   - `blocking` inside `extern "C" { }`.
3. **Expressions the list omits:**
   - `lends self`, the borrowing-struct initializer's proof;
   - prefix `*p` (design 219);
   - the call-site `&x` and `&var x` sigils, and the `(&x) as UnsafePointer<T>` crossing;
   - `try(as E.Case) f()` routing (design 234);
   - `#file`, `#line` and `#function`;
   - `not`, and the wrapping operators `&+`, `&-` and `&*`, for the precedence table;
   - a tuple index chain, `t.0.1` (design 161);
   - `sizeof<T>()` and `alignof<T>()`;
   - `if var`, `guard var` and `while var`.
4. **Disambiguation points to add to §6:**
   - **`{`:** a block, a closure, a Map or Set literal (`{:}`, `{1, 2}`), or an interpolation. `{}` and `{expr}` are always closures.
   - **The `borrow let|var` prefix's extent and precedence.** SL:borrowing §2.2 says it covers the place "up to and including its `borrows` call", and that is a *type* fact, while the tree records syntax only. So the grammar should give the prefix a purely syntactic operand, a postfix expression, and state how it binds against `??`, the binary operators and `=`. Cases: `borrow let doc.section_at(x).get("k") ?? ""` and `borrow var v[i].x = borrow let v[j].x`. The split at the last `borrows` call is typecheck's.
   - **`sizeof`/`alignof` written bare in a generic-argument list** (`Ring<sizeof<UInt64>()>`, DF-307a), and why `1 << 8` cannot appear there (design 185).
   - **Prefix `*` versus multiplication** (`a * *p`), and a tuple index never taking a following `.`.
5. **The context list misses the positions design 224 calls "a container's head",** which is where many of the Python compiler's position bugs lived:
   - the `for` head: either range endpoint, or the iterator;
   - the `if let`/`guard let`/`while let` subject;
   - the right side of `&&`, `||` and `??`.

   Also missing:
   - the `borrow` head, the `lend` operand, the `try` subject, and a catch body;
   - a collection-literal element, a struct-literal field value, and an enum payload argument;
   - an assignment target, a compound assignment's right side, and a `break` value;
   - a capture list;
   - attribute arguments (`@align(N)`, `@test(refuses: "…")`);
   - the const positions: an array length and a repeat count.
6. **Sources: add SL:architecture §3.1 and §3.2.** Those sections hold the lexer and parser contracts:
   - the tree records syntax only;
   - one `Call` node;
   - flat operator and `else if` lists;
   - postfix chains nested and charged per hop;
   - an interpolation as literal segments plus token ranges;
   - newlines as tokens;
   - contextual words stay identifiers.

   §6 paraphrases some of these, but the AST node kinds that U2b turns into the dump contract have to follow them.
