**v0.24.4's `SpinLock.acquire` makes a contended heap ~14× slower under QEMU riscv32, with the yield never taken.**

sawos's `arena_race` (two threads of one process allocating from one arena under a fast tick, each allocation inside std's `SpinLock`) runs its ELF in **0.7 s at v0.24.3 and 9.5–20 s at v0.24.4** under `qemu-system-riscv32`. At the default job count it passes 10/10 at v0.24.3 and 2/10 at v0.24.4 (10 s harness timeout).

**Isolated to `std/spinlock.saw`** (all ELFs timed directly under QEMU):

| variant | wall |
|---|---|
| v0.24.3 | 0.7 s |
| v0.24.4, sosrt's strong `__saw_rt_thread_yield` | 9.5–10 s |
| v0.24.4, the seam removed (std's weak default) | 10.1 s |
| v0.24.4 with `SPINS_BEFORE_YIELD = 1e9` (the yield never reached) | 9.7–10.3 s |
| v0.24.4 toolchain with only `std/spinlock.saw` replaced by v0.24.3's | **0.7 s** |

So the yield itself is not the cost: the counter and the call in the acquire loop are. The heap, `alloc` and `highest_bit` code is byte-identical between the fast and slow builds.

**What we measured:**
- Timer interrupts over a run: about 6,400 at v0.24.4 against about 440 at v0.24.3 and in the swapped-file build. That is roughly one tick per allocation, about 6,000 allocations.
- The wall time does not move with the tick period (100, 137 and 1000 µs all take about 9.7 s).
- Sampling the lock's words finds it held in 44 of 45 samples, with the owner alternating between the two workers. About 40% of PC samples are in the acquire loop.

**Not established:** why the new loop shape costs one stall per allocation when the hold times are unchanged. An instruction trace changes the timing too much to use.

A guess worth checking: the loop's lr/sc (or its compare-exchange) has changed shape, so QEMU's TCG takes its exclusive-step atomic path on each failed attempt. If so, the slowdown is an emulator effect, but it is still on every sawos gate run.
