## Description

**Two rule questions about optional chains, for the project owner. Found by the reviewer while probing SL-1119.p1 (Nov 14); neither is a defect of that patch, and each is the same on main apart from what the patch newly lets build.** The reviewer's probes are `x19a_move_chain_timing.saw` and the generic one in `.build/scratch/air-review/sl1119_p1/agent/probes/` on its machine (ask in chat).

**Q1. `(move o)?.label` does not consume `o` at the statement.** The chain reads in place, and `o` is dropped at the end of its scope, not where it was moved. Options: (a) a `move` at a chain's head consumes the Optional at that statement (the chain's value is read out of a temporary, which is then dropped), which is what `move` means everywhere else; (b) today's behaviour is the rule, and the spec says that a `move` at a chain's head is a no-op for timing. **Lead recommends (a).**

**Q2. A generic body moves a `NoCopy` final field out of a chain, and the same chain written without the generic is refused.** `func first<T>(...) -> T? { cell(...)?.value }` at a `NoCopy` `T` builds (since SL-1119; before it, it stopped at SL-1119's internal error) and moves the value out of the temporary, with every deinit running once. `make(true)?.t` with a `NoCopy` field `t` is refused by `transfer.implicit-copy`, the rule that a chain's final field must be copyable (design 111). Options: (a) the generic body is refused too, at its declaration, for want of a `Copy` bound on `T` (a place read in a generic body needs a `Copy` bound: the design-141 rule); (b) moving the final field out of a chain whose head is an owned temporary is allowed for every type, generic or not, since nothing else names the temporary, and the copyable-final-field rule narrows to a chain whose head is a place. **Lead recommends (b)**: it is what the lowering already does soundly, and it removes a refusal with no safety reason behind it; but it changes a design-111 sentence, so it is the owner's.

Until answered, nothing changes: the generic spelling builds and is sound by the reviewer's deinit counts, and the plain spelling is refused.
