## Description

**Two rule questions about optional chains, for the project owner. Found by the reviewer while probing SL-1119.p1 (Nov 14); neither is a defect of that patch, and each is the same on main apart from what the patch newly lets build.** The reviewer's probes are `x19a_move_chain_timing.saw` and the generic one in `.build/scratch/air-review/sl1119_p1/agent/probes/` on its machine (ask in chat).

**Q1. `(move o)?.label` does not consume `o` at the statement.** The chain reads in place, and `o` is dropped at the end of its scope, not where it was moved. Options: (a) a `move` at a chain's head consumes the Optional at that statement (the chain's value is read out of a temporary, which is then dropped), which is what `move` means everywhere else; (b) today's behaviour is the rule, and the spec says that a `move` at a chain's head is a no-op for timing. **Lead recommends (a).**

**Q2. A generic body moves a `NoCopy` final field out of a chain, and the same chain written without the generic is refused.** `func first<T>(...) -> T? { cell(...)?.value }` at a `NoCopy` `T` builds (since SL-1119; before it, it stopped at SL-1119's internal error) and moves the value out of the temporary, with every deinit running once. `make(true)?.t` with a `NoCopy` field `t` is refused by `transfer.implicit-copy`, the rule that a chain's final field must be copyable (design 111). Options: (a) the generic body is refused too, at its declaration, for want of a `Copy` bound on `T` (a place read in a generic body needs a `Copy` bound: the design-141 rule); (b) moving the final field out of a chain whose head is an owned temporary is allowed for every type, generic or not, since nothing else names the temporary, and the copyable-final-field rule narrows to a chain whose head is a place. **Lead recommends (b)**: it is what the lowering already does soundly, and it removes a refusal with no safety reason behind it; but it changes a design-111 sentence, so it is the owner's.

Until answered, nothing changes: the generic spelling builds and is sound by the reviewer's deinit counts, and the plain spelling is refused.

**The sweep is done (lead, Nov 15; a read-only Sonnet sweep on main at c8d89f0d8; its report is in the lead's transcript and its evidence in `.build/scratch/p1126s/` in the lead's checkout). Then the implementation detail: two units, U1 first.**

**What the sweep found.**
- **The lost `move` is wider than a chain's head, and it is also SL-1125.** `(move x)` as the base of any data-field read (`.f`, `!.f`, `?.f`, `.0`, `[i].f` on a fixed array) is read in place: no move is recorded, `x` stays usable (`let n = (move o)?.n; let m = o?.n` builds), and `x` is dropped at the end of its scope, not the statement. When the field read takes an OWNING field (`(move p).s`, `.t`, `!.s`, a tuple's `.0`), the build stops with `INVARIANT ... local _1 is dropped whole after a part of it moved out`: SL-1125's smallest form is `let p = mkp(); let r = (move p).s` with `struct Ps1 { s: String }`, no Optional needed. Also: `(move x).bump()` with a `&var self` method builds on a `let x` and mutates it, `x` usable after; `(move o)?.finish()` (a consuming method through a hop) is an INVARIANT; in a generic body `(move c).value` is an INVARIANT at every bound.
- **Where `move` already works:** `let y = move x`, `f(move x)`, `(move x) ?? d`, `match move x`, `if let p = move x`, and a `&self` or consuming method on a plain base (`(move x).peek()`): each consumes at the statement and a later use is `move.use-after`.
- **The mechanism (lowering, `compiler/mir/src/lower.saw`):** `move_val` moves the place into a statement temporary only when the move node's use is `TcUse.Borrow` (`moved_to_temporary`, the fix SL-643 / ledger row V141 made for format arguments, comparison operands, borrowed receivers and shared arguments). Every other use returns the local's own place (`MirVal(place: p, fresh: false)`). Typecheck gives the base of a member read `TcUse.Project` (`member_expr`, `optional_member_expr`, `chain_hop`, `tuple_index`, `force_expr`, `subscript_expr`, `field_call`, `borrow_place`) and a `&var` receiver `BorrowVar`, so for those the `move` is lost: the read goes through the local itself, `take_from_temporary` finds no temporary root (`temporary_root` wants a `Temp` local), and for an owning field the part is moved out of a local still dropped whole.
- **The chain rule (Q2) lives in one branch:** `optional_chain` (`compiler/typecheck/src/bodies.saw`), `field && not self.copies_silently(ft)` → `refuse_chain_field`. A chain's top is a place exactly when its base is (`optional_member_expr`: `place = expr_place[base] || referent(bt) >= 0`). The lowering already moves a field out of a temporary for `!.` and plain `.` (`take_from_temporary`), except where the temporary's own type writes a `deinit`: `slice.not-yet` ("a consuming destructure of a type that writes its own `deinit` is not lowered to MIR in the bootstrap slice yet"), the open question the MIR lane's `refuse/consuming-deinit*.saw` fixtures record.
- **Generic bodies:** a place head over an unbounded `T` is not refused by the chain rule (`copies_silently` answers yes for a type parameter) but by the call-site requirement design 219 infers (`copy.requirement` at the call); a temporary head builds for every `T`. Q2 changes neither.
- **Consumers:** no tracked `.saw` uses `(move x)?.` or `(move x)!.`; `(move x).field` appears once, in conformance V104 (`print("move {}", (move r).w)`), whose expected output already says the value is dropped at that statement: `corpus_diff.tsv` records it `wrong` today and U1 flips it right. Four tracked files are refused by the chain rule; two have a temporary head and flip with U2 (`compiler/tests/typecheck/refuse/transfer.implicit-copy.chain-field-temporary.saw`, `tests/corpus/errors/optional_chain_nocopy_field.saw`).
