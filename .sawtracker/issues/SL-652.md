**Convert the compiler's constructor-shaped factory functions to `init`s and `static func`s** (the user's Oct 15 ruling; the rule is the sibling issue). Mechanical, with no behaviour change. The user approved a Sonnet `mech` agent for it.

**Census (the lead, Oct 15, `.build/scratch/probe_factories2.py`):** 71 top-level free functions in `compiler/*/src` return a struct the compiler declares, over 45 types. None are generic.
- **32 pure constructors**, whose body is one literal of the returned type. These are converted:
  - into a labeled `init` (`tc_value(...)` → `TcValue(value: …)`, `tc_fault(f)` → `TcValue(fault: f)`);
  - into a `static func` where there is no argument to label (`res_unbound()` → `ResBinding.unbound()`, `borrowck_no_path()`, `target_blank()`, `tc_write_ok()`);
  - or deleted in favour of the memberwise construction, where the parameters are exactly the fields (`eval_value(kind:bits:ty:)`, if so).
- **39 compute-then-build functions** stay functions (`coro_edges`, `coro_interference`, `parse_source`, `ll_format_pieces`). The stage entry points (`mir_program(tc)`, `borrowck_program(mir)`, `coro_program(mono)`, …) stay too; converting them is out of scope.

**Grids the brief must pin:**
- the per-function target, decided by the lead before dispatch: `init` label, `static func` name, or memberwise;
- the no-guessing rule: a function whose target isn't in the table is left alone and listed as OPEN;
- every call site converted in the same commit as its function.

**Gate:** `compiler/tests/run.py --only` for every stage touched, plus `subset` and `citations`. Behaviour is byte-identical: no golden, MIR, IR or run output changes, and the fixpoint lane (server) confirms Stage 1 and Stage 2 agree.

**Timing:** at a quiet point, after the coroutine units in flight land, since the rename touches call sites in all 12 stages; or folded into SL-425's post-self-hosting re-canonicalization pass.

**Air, for the per-function grid:** "deleted in favour of the memberwise construction" works only where every caller can see every field. The memberwise construction is visible only where all the fields are (SL-648's own note), and a compiler struct's fields are private outside their defining module unless declared `public`. So a field-for-field factory that another module calls has no legal target in compiler source:
- the memberwise construction is invisible at that call site;
- an exact-match `init` is an override under SL-648, which Stage 0 refuses (SL-651's compiler-source limit).

Suggestion: for each row whose target is "memberwise", the lead's table also records whether every call site is in the struct's defining module. Where one isn't, the row's target is "stays a function" (or, if the fields are meant to be public, "make the fields `public`, then memberwise"), never a silent guess, under the brief's OPEN rule. `eval_value(kind:bits:ty:)` is worth checking first, since it's the example the census names.

**Air: a correction to my c2, which was wrong on two counts.**

1. **The visibility rule is narrower than I wrote.** The spec ("Cross-module memberwise construction", in the visibility section) says a `public struct` whose fields are bare is memberwise-constructible from anywhere. Only a field marked `private` hides it. I'd taken CLAUDE.md's design-80 digest, "private-by-default", without checking the spec. So for the compiler's public structs with bare fields, "memberwise" works at every call site. The constraint applies only to a struct with a `private` field, or a non-public struct whose factory is called from another module. The grid needs a check only for those rows.
2. **`eval_value(kind:bits:ty:)` is not field-for-field.** It fills in `decl: -1` and `fault: TcFault.Clean` (`compiler/eval/src/program.saw`), so its target is a labelled `init(kind:bits:ty:)`, a subset of the fields and an ordinary overload under SL-648, not the memberwise construction. It has 10 call sites in `eval/src/interp.saw`.

**Lead, for the per-function grid** (the Air's SL-651 nit, chat m564): a constructor taking exactly the fields becomes the memberwise construction only if every caller can see every field. If the struct has a `private` field and any caller is outside its module, there's no memberwise construction there.
