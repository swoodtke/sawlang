**SL-571.p1 submitted (A13a; agent p571 then p571b, one commit).**

**Surface:**
- `std/channel.saw` (new module `std.channel`) holds `Channel<T: Send>`, a `Copy` handle with an atomic refcount and the runtime's one-word lock. It provides `init`, `send`, `close`, `try_receive` and `receive`, plus `ChannelError` (`Closed`, `Cancelled`, `Alloc(e:)`, `Printable` + `Error`), with `///` docs throughout.
- Left out: the blocking `recv` (the thread engine, out of scope) and Stage 0's transform-only helpers.
- Divergences from Stage 0: the runtime lock in place of a pthread mutex and condition variable, and an out-of-memory panic that reports the node's or block's own `sizeof`.

**Readiness:**
- `publish_ready` is the one writer of the invariant (`ready != 0` exactly when `count > 0` or `closed`). Its entry points are `close`, `enqueue` and `take`.
- `receive` checks `cancelled()` at the top of its loop, before reading the queue.

- `park_wakes` wakes a channel park once its readiness word is nonzero. Its entry points are `TaskGroup.runnable`, `should_stop` and `idle`.
- `waits_on` accepts a channel park.
- `idle` panics with an interim message naming A13b when only unwoken channel parks are left (the lead's ruling under the deferral rule; K67 waits on SL-572).
- A channel park registers nothing, so there is nothing to withdraw.

Approved patch SL-571.p1 (revision 2)

**Air: SL-571.p1 r2 approved.** r2's +/- lines are identical to r1's. Built from the seed on origin/main 5983188f3 (with SL-731) + r2. Probes are in `air-review/sl571p/` on the Air.

**`std/channel.saw` against the spec** (Channels; "Disconnection is EXPLICIT"; `try_` means non-blocking):
- `close` wakes every waiter, and a second close is `Err(Closed)`.
- A send after close is `Err(Closed)`, and it destroys its value.
- `receive` drains before `Closed`, and checks `cancelled()` at its loop top, ahead of the queue read.
- `try_receive` is `Ok(None)` on an open, empty channel and `Err(Closed)` once closed and drained.
- The last handle destroys what is still queued.
- `publish_ready` is the one writer of the readiness word. A receiver parks after an unlocked empty `take`, but the wake is level-triggered on the word, so a send in between is not lost.

**Behaviour** (`own.saw`, owning `NoCopy` messages with a printing `deinit`): byte-identical to Stage 0 and -O0 = -O2, 0 leaks under `leaks --atExit`, clean under Guard Malloc at both levels. It covers:
- a late send dropping `late` before `Err(Closed)`;
- close keeping two queued messages, one taken after close and one destroyed by the last handle;
- `try_receive`'s three answers;
- a cross-task `receive` woken by each send and by the close;
- a parked `receive` cancelled, then sent to before it resumes: `Err(Cancelled)`, nothing dequeued, the message destroyed when the last handle drops.

Also:
- `Channel<Raw>` with a raw-pointer payload is refused at `init` (`type.bound`, `T: Send`).
- A receive nothing can wake ends in the ruled interim panic naming A13b. Stage 0 prints the full deadlock report, which is A13b's.

**Gates** (on main with SL-731):
- `corpusdiff.py gate`: all 2661 programs hold their baselines.
- `run.py --only subset,eval,borrowck,drops,mono`: ok. These are the steps your list left out while the loader changed.
- gmgate: the two channel programs move from Blocked to twins, and no Blocked entry still waits on `std.channel` or SL-571.

**Notes, not blocking:**
1. **`idle`'s panic and `park_wakes`'s plain read are single-threaded assumptions.** The `idle` comment says "A channel's readiness word is set only by code this thread runs". K56 (registered on SL-765) has a `Thread.spawn` producer feeding main's `receive`, so under SL-765 `idle` would panic while the producer is still running: it must wait instead. Under SL-578's pool, `park_wakes` reads `ready` with a plain load while `publish_ready` writes it under the lock, so the read needs an acquire load. I'll note both on those issues.
2. `try_receive` is declared `unsafe` with nothing unsafe in its body. That's the lead's nit, copied from Stage 0's std signature. `close` and `init` do need it, since each binds the block pointer.
