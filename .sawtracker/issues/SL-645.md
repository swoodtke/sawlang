## Description

**A drive point panics on a non-ready park (A4b's interim, SL-636). A11 replaces the panic.**

A4b's test drivers `__saw_drive` and `__saw_drive_steps` run their root's frame through std's `drive_frame` (`std/taskgroup.saw`). A ready yield is resumed at once (t8). Any other park panics, naming the reason and the drive site:

`` `__saw_drive(...)` at FILE:LINE:COL: the driven frame is parked on a timer, which a drive point does not wait on yet ``

**Why it's a panic and not a handoff to A7b's sweep:** the sweep (`exec_run`) resumes only slots whose park word is `PARK_READY`. Nothing in A7b's executor ever turns a non-ready park word back to ready: there is no timer list, no reactor poll and no channel wake. Its one answer to any other park is `refuse_parked`'s panic. Where the frame lives doesn't matter: a caller-stack frame can sit in a group slot with `owned: false`, as `__saw_exec_drive_main` does with `main`'s. The missing piece is the wait itself.

**Replace it at A11 (SL-568, timers):** the drive point's non-ready park hands its wait to the shared sweep and the idle wait. Other tasks run meanwhile, and the drive resumes once its own park word is ready again, as in Stage 0 ("one sweep ... shared by every drive point", `sawc/std/taskgroup.saw`). A12a's fd park and A13a's channel wait then reach drive points through the same handoff.

**Pin:** `compiler/tests/std/exec/drive_timer_park.saw` drives a hand-built frame that parks on a timer and expects the panic. It is a `coro.not-yet`-style loud not-yet, never a spin. Today no lowered frame can reach it, because a non-ready park isn't lowered until A3c (SL-556).
