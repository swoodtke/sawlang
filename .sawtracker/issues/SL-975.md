## Description

**`tests/corpus/conformance/K90_thread_detach_is_a_fate.saw` depends on thread timing, and failed a corpusdiff gate under load.** Seen in SL-970 unit 5's gate at a load average of 12–27: `'sawc meets its directives' -> 'sawc: the output does not contain 'ticket released' in order'`.

**Evidence** (`.build/scratch/p970f/`):
- The IR is byte-identical between the two compilers compared.
- 10 of 10 direct runs pass, and 5 of 5 `corpusdiff.py run` re-measures hold. Only the loaded gate run failed.

**Theory (UNVERIFIED):** the program waits for two detached threads by polling with a 5 s limit. Under heavy load, a thread doesn't finish inside it, so its `ticket released` line is missing.

**Direction:** the program must state its guarantee (a detached thread's fate) without a wall-clock deadline. For example, wait on a channel or an atomic the threads signal, with no timeout, or one far longer than any load could need. The EXPECT text stays the same. Check the other corpus and run-lane programs that poll with a deadline (obligation 4) and list them. Record any verdict move with `corpusdiff.py run --baseline`.

**SL-975.p1: corpus and probe waits that load could break**

- **K90:** its cause was a race in the program, not the 5 s deadline. The ticket's deinit raised the count before it printed, with a non-atomic load-then-store, so main could exit while the second thread was still printing. Under load the original failed 1 of 1,000 and 2 of 2,000 runs. Now it prints first, counts with one `fetch_add`, and waits under a 20 s deadline that ends in a panic. It failed 0 of 2,000.
- **Polls with a deadline:** these widen to TESTING.md rule 3's deadline, each ending in a panic that names what never arrived:
  - 20 s for corpus programs: K48, K49, K51, K52, K204 and `taskgroup_mt_live_pool_soak`;
  - 10 s for probes: the soak's probe copy and `net_connect_reads_its_outcome_from_the_socket`.
- **Sleep once, then read once:** these become condition waits under the same bound.
  - K50 waits on its flag;
  - `taskgroup_slot_reuse_o_live` waits on `group.count() == 0`;
  - `taskgroup_result_unjoined_once` waits on a flag the producer sets in its last turn.
- **What doesn't change:** every EXPECT and `// prints:` line.
- **Pins:** each changed program ran 200 times, 12 at once plus 6 CPU burners, at a 1-minute load of 120–165. Every run gave its stated output, including both probes at -O0 and -O2.
- **corpusdiff:** exactly the 11 changed corpus programs moved, all to a pass judged by their own directives. Three of them, K90, K204 and the soak, left `stage0-wrong`.
- **Gates:** `run.py --only programs`: ok; the corpusdiff gate: 2,675 programs hold.

The timer-ordered and measured-margin programs from the sweep are SL-977.

Approved patch SL-975.p1 (revision 1)

**Air: SL-975.p1, approved.**

**Read:**
- **K90's fix is the right one.** The deinit now prints before it counts, and counts with one `fetch_add`. So once main reads two releases, both lines are written, and two threads releasing at once can't lose a count. The thread's spin can go unbounded, because main always opens `GO` before it waits, or panics.
- **Each wait now follows TESTING.md rule 3:** it polls the state it's about to assert on, under a deadline of seconds, and fails with a panic that names what never arrived.
- **The three condition waits keep their claims:**
  - **K50:** the loop ends only if the pool task ran while main slept or read the flag; otherwise it panics at 20 s.
  - **`taskgroup_slot_reuse_o_live`:** if slots weren't reclaimed, `count()` would never reach 0, and the wave wait panics instead of printing peak 192. The `final 0` line is now guaranteed by that loop, which is harmless.
  - **`taskgroup_result_unjoined_once`:** its group is cooperative, so nothing runs between the producer's `FINISHING.store(1)` and its return, and `held` is released in that same turn.
- **corpusdiff:** exactly the 11 edited programs move, all to pass, judged by their own directives. SL-765 and SL-769 are still cited by other rows (14 and 3).
