**Split from SL-489** (it was SL:open-questions O4). This is a design question for the lead to settle with the Air. It is reversible, and is decided when the new std grows a `Map`.

SL:borrowing §2.6 says collections implement `LendingIterator`, but it doesn't name a `Map` iterator: there is no method name and no item shape. A map entry is a key and a value. The item would be one of:
- a tuple of places, which §7's tuple lends cover as a separate rule;
- a pair struct, which is a borrowing struct holding `&K` and `&V` or `&var V`.

The spec's Map "Iteration" section currently says `Map` declares no iterator yet, and that its visitors and snapshots are its iteration forms.

**Owed when this is decided:**
- the method names (shared and exclusive);
- the item shape, and whether the key is ever writable (it must not be, since a write would change the hash);
- the `for borrow let (k, v) in m` spelling, if the tuple form is chosen;
- the spec text, and a D-entry in SL:open-questions.

Carrying over the two prerequisites from my comment on O4 (SL:open-questions t20). They decide whether the tuple shape is even available.

§7, as ruled, says a tuple lend is "under one exclusive root charge", and its examples are all-`&var` tuples (`split_at`, `pair`, `cells`). A map entry needs the key read-only in *both* modes, since a key write breaks the hash invariant. So the tuple shape needs two things §7 doesn't yet say:
1. **A mixed tuple lend,** `(&K, &var V)`, for `for borrow var (k, v) in m`.
2. **A shared root charge for an all-`&` tuple lend** from a `(&self) borrows` accessor, `(&K, &V)`. Under §7's text as written it would hold the map exclusively, so a read-only loop would block every other reader of `m`.

Both look like "yes" by analogy with §3's table, but they extend a Ruled section, so they need a ruling before the tuple form can be chosen. The pair-struct form (a borrowing struct with a read-only `key` and a `&V` / `&var V` value) needs neither, and is the alternative if they aren't granted.
