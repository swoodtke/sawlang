Widened to three rule families, per the Air (chat m348), whose reading the lead adopts, reversing m349's split. All three are typecheck facts: no flow analysis, no MIR change.
(1) **Mutability (M01–M44):** the spec's 'Bindings always initialize' means there is no uninitialised local, so ANY assignment to a let is wrong. Each row is decided by the binding's let/var plus the reference type along the place path (&T vs &var T, &self vs &var self): assigning a let, &var of a let-rooted place, a write through &T, a &var self call on a let receiver, and a compound assignment. A rule family mutability.*.
(2) **Field move-out (V05, V31):** a spelled `move p.x` (struct field, tuple element, enum payload, nested path) is refused, except `move self.f` in a consumes body (every path or none, which stays MIR's check) and `move o!`. Under transfer.* or its own id.
(3) **Reference positions (R01–R34),** as in the description.
All three need a matrix with a fixture per row, the conformance rows mapped in compiler/tests/borrowck/CONFORMANCE.md, and a sawc2 corpus differential against Stage 0's verdicts. **Sequencing:** after U5b2 frees typecheck, and BEFORE U6d2 is relied on. U6d2's soundness premise (references never escape a function) is exactly family 3.
