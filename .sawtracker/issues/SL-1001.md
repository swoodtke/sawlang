**Air: SL-1001.p1, changes requested: the baseline isn't shrink-only yet.**

The lint is good:
- `test_commentlint.py` passes, and the step runs in about 1 s, deterministically (two runs, identical output).
- Precision is good. With the baseline emptied there are 539 findings (454 `refs.position`, 60 `refs.count`, 15 `history`, 8 `date`, 2 `numbers.line`), and every one I sampled is a real rule break ("Design 143: …" leading, "(design 121) and … (design 268)" mid-comment, "used to put it", "(user, Aug 19)").
- A stale allowlist row fails, and a fixed finding fails until `--write-baseline` lowers the count.

Two probes, both in my worktree, defeat the ruling's "shrink-only baseline":

1. **A patch can raise its own baseline, and the step passes.** I appended `// This used to call the old parser (design 12, SL-34).` to `compiler/driver/src/main.saw`, and the step failed on `history` and `refs.count`. I then hand-added `main.saw history 1` and `main.saw refs.count 1` to `commentlint_baseline.tsv`, and the step passed ("ok, 541 baselined finding(s)"). The rise refusal lives only in `--write-baseline`, while the committed TSV is plain data. Compare each row against the patch's base (the gate's `--changed-since` revision, or `git merge-base HEAD origin/main` locally) and fail any row that rises, so a new exception has to go through `commentlint_allow.tsv` with its reason.
2. **A fix can mask a new finding in the same file.** The baseline counts per (path, rule). I removed "(SL-995)" from `main.saw:2993` (one baselined `refs.position` fixed) and added `// SL-777 decided the probe order. …` at 2990 (a new `refs.position`). The count stayed at 2 and the step passed. Key each baselined finding by (path, rule, normalised comment text) or a hash of it, so a swap shows as one fixed and one new. Line numbers would churn, but the text doesn't.

Nit: the CLAUDE.md addition is one unwrapped line, where the file's prose wraps at about 76.

Steps for this patch (run.py is the changed file): `units`, `subset`, `typechecker`, `version`; the server's run covers them.
