Found by SL-865 U6c (p865c), the first riscv32 execution of mono's hash glue. On riscv32, an `Int64`/`UInt64` hash takes only its LOW 32 bits (the glue does `from(truncating:)` to the word), so every key sharing a low word collides: `value`, `value + 2^32` and `0x5678` all hash to `691574859` on riscv32, but to three different digests on arm64. Probe: `.build/scratch/p865/u6c/hash_probe.saw`.

The hash/== contract still holds, but this contradicts the prelude's stated property that a digest doesn't depend on the platform word, and it degrades a `Map<Int64, _>` or `Set<Int64>` on a 32-bit target.

The fix: mono's hash glue feeds a wider-than-word integer to the hasher as both halves (or as 8 bytes), identically on every target, so the digest matches across platforms. Pin: the probe's three values give the same three digests on riscv32 and arm64 (the freestanding suite's `wide_hash_format` case can assert them).

**Implementation detail (lead, Nov 12): mono's hash glue feeds a wider-than-word integer to the hasher as two words, low then high, so a digest is the same on every target.**

**Where.** `compiler/mono/src/glue.saw`, `hash_word(value, t)`: "a width of the target's word as it is, a wider one its low word (which equal values still share), a narrower one zero-extended." On riscv32 an `Int64`/`UInt64` therefore hashes by its low 32 bits: `v`, `v + 2^32` and `0x5678` (with `v`'s low word `0x5678`) collide, and the digest differs from arm64's, against the prelude's statement that a digest doesn't depend on the platform word.

**Decision: the digest of an integer is defined over its VALUE's 64-bit two-word form on every target.** Concretely, each integer feeds the hasher two 32-bit halves, low then high, whatever the target's word:
- That is the only definition a 32-bit and a 64-bit target can share without 64-bit arithmetic in the 32-bit glue.
- It changes today's digests on 64-bit targets too if they currently feed one 64-bit word. Whether that is acceptable depends on what the hasher's `write`-style entry takes: read the glue's callers of `hash_word` and the std hasher (`std/hash.saw` or where `Hashable`'s streaming type lives) FIRST, and report which of these holds before changing anything:
  - (a) the hasher takes word-sized writes and mixes per write: then splitting changes 64-bit digests. STOP after the census and report; the lead takes "digests change on 64-bit targets" to the user, since programs may have recorded digests (the run lane's `aggregate_hash`, `float_hook_hash`, `wide_hash_format` do).
  - (b) the hasher has, or can be given, a 64-bit entry that is itself defined as two 32-bit steps: then a 64-bit target's digests are unchanged and only the 32-bit target's wide-integer digests move, to match. Implement that: the glue passes a wider-than-word integer as its low word then its high word (`from(truncating:)` of the value, and of the value shifted right by the word's bits), through the hasher's existing per-word step, in that order.

**Census (evidence first, in the evidence directory).** The issue's probe shape, built for the host and for `riscv32-unknown-none-elf` (the freestanding runner's way of running a riscv32 case; read `tests/freestanding/README.md` and `wide_hash_format.saw`):
