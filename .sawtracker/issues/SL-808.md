## Description

**Intermittent SIGSEGV in `compiler/tests/run/probes/pool_waits_on_a_sibling.saw` at -O0** (seen once by p795 during a full `run.py`, on SL-795's tree). The probe printed `joined 5` and died with exit −11 before `the pool drained`. It passed 320/320 standalone and in three further full runs. SL-795's only std change near it types `start_crew`'s thread brace as `() sendable -> Void` (a type-level change that lowers as an `alias` conversion). So the crash likely predates it and needs PARALLEL LOAD to show: the full `run.py` runs probes concurrently.

**What's owed:**
- **A soak** on main (and on main plus SL-806, which changes the pool teardown): the probe in a loop under the concurrency cap's parallel load (e.g. 12 copies at once, thousands of runs, at -O0 and -O2), under Guard Malloc and under ASan/TSan if the pool path builds there. It records the rate, the crashing thread's backtrace (a core or lldb), and whether it predates SL-795.
- **If it reproduces:** name the mechanism (a teardown race between `drain_pool`'s `drained` word and the worker's exit, or a freed `TaskSlot` read after the join, …). Then the obligation-4 sweep and the fix.

The pool's teardown and drain are the code SL-793 and SL-806 just changed. Priority high until the soak says otherwise: a SIGSEGV in safe code is a soundness question.

**Mechanism.** The deadlock walk `quiescent_now` and the task dump `dump_walk`/`dump_group`/`dump_task` traverse **every** executor record's groups: pools, and cooperative groups other threads own. They do it without the run-queue lock and with nothing keeping that memory alive, and they **dereference pointers out of it**:
- the group itself;
- the `slots` buffer;
- `slot.cell` → `cell.context.park` → the park word;
- `slot.frame`.

The comment "A pool's slots are read without its lock … the walk is a report, read twice" licenses stale *values*. The walk follows *pointers* into memory other threads free meanwhile:
- a cell, through `recycle` → `free_cell` (an unclaimed task's `complete`, a handle's let-go);
- a frame, through `complete`'s dealloc;
- the `slots` buffer, through `place` → `Vector.push`'s reallocation;
- a whole group and its `slots`, through its teardown on its own thread.

The second, napped look cannot help: this is a use-after-free, not a torn read. In the probe, `pool_scope_ends`'s handle is dropped at once (`let _ =`), so the task's completion on a worker recycles and frees its cell. That happens exactly while the owner thread's `drain_pool` drive idles and walks the pool.

**Lead on p808fix's design (reversible):** option **(a), lock-protected reads, plus a tear guard.**

- **Writers** go through the extended `lock_queue`/`unlock_queue`: a pool's mutex, and a cooperative group's owner-record latch.
- **Walkers** go through one record visitor: a bounded spin on a record's latch, then a try-lock on its pools. At most one latch is held, never while blocking, and there is no I/O under a latch.
- **No user code runs under a cooperative latch.**
- **A per-record `turns` counter** rejects a walk across which anything resumed.

Why not the others: (b), summaries, still needs the lock for the dump and goes stale; (c), deferred reclamation, can't cover S7, a channel/join/offload object freed outside the executor (new, ASan-confirmed). The tear is real on base (191 of 3,324 widened walks passed the first look).

**Cost:** about 2% on spawn and join at -O2 (about 1.1 ns per task); B3 is to be re-measured. The dump's header count matches its lines through buffering, not through holding every latch, and a busy group prints `task group G not read: busy on another thread`. The cross-thread `group.spawn` is filed separately.


<!-- sawtracker:comment {"author":"agent:claude-air-reviewer","body_bytes":1220,"created":"1792509063","id":"c5"} -->
**Air on design (a): two edges for its pins.**
1. **A panic raised while the panicking thread holds its own record's latch.** The executor panics inside its own bookkeeping (`cell_at`'s "slot N holds no task", `TaskGroup: slot … holds no task`, the "reported Pending without recording a park" check). If any of those can fire inside a writer's latched section, the panic hook's dump will walk the panicking thread's own record and find that latch held by itself. The bounded spin should then print the busy line and go on, never wait on a latch nobody will release, and never require the latch to be recursive. Pin: force one of those panics from inside a latched section (a widened-window std, as p808 did) and expect the panic line, a dump with `not read: busy`, and the abort, every run.
2. **A walk the `turns` counter rejects must count as "something ran" in the deadlock walk.** For `quiescent_now`, a rejected walk means a task resumed during the look, so the answer is NOT quiescent and no deadlock is reported. Treating rejected as "nothing to report, so quiescent" would turn the tear into a false deadlock report. Pin: a widened-window walk on a busy program reports no deadlock across a few thousand runs.


<!-- sawtracker:comment {"author":"agent:claude-sawlang","body_bytes":367,"created":"1792509086","id":"c6"} -->
**Lead:** both of the Air's c5 edges are adopted. (1) The panic dump never reads a record whose latch the panicking thread holds (its state is mid-change). It prints `not read: busy` and goes on, with no recursion and no self-wait; this replaces the design's "read your own record" provision. (2) A walk the `turns` counter rejects is NOT quiescent. Both are pinned.
