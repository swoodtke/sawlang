**Found by:** the Air, reviewing SL-505.p1 (U7a). Repros `f2_min.saw`, `d8_matchtime.saw` and `e9_matchconsume.saw` are in chat file f22 (m454). **Area:** MIR's match lowering, and a spec gap. **Needs a user ruling.**

**The divergence:**
- In a consumed `match`, `case Full(_) -> print("arm")` prints `arm` and then `drop 3` in sawc2, but `drop 3` and then `arm` in Stage 0.
- sawc2 also disagrees with itself: an arm that binds part of the payload drops its `_` parts at arm entry.
- On an owned `Noisy?`, sawc2 drops at the arm's end and Stage 0 at the scope's end. There, sawc2's behaviour reads like the spec's "match consumes an owned enum".

**The gap:** the spec doesn't say when a discarded (`_`) payload, or an unbound part of one, is dropped. The candidates are arm entry, arm end and scope end. A ruling is needed before MIR picks one; then the spec states it and both match paths follow it.

**Ruled by the user (Oct 10): option A, arm entry.** What a consumed `match` does not bind (a `_` sub-pattern, a pattern that binds nothing, a `case _` arm) is destroyed at arm entry, before the arm body runs. This is the meaning `_` already has in `let _ = e` and `let (a, _) = pair`. Arm bindings still die at the arm's end. Recorded in SL:open-questions (Resolved, SL-513). The order among several discarded fields is my scope decision, not part of the ruling: reverse declaration order.

**The work:**
- the spec: one sentence in "Match consumes an owned enum";
- sawc2: MIR match lowering, so an arm that binds nothing destroys the scrutinee at arm entry, as the partial-binding path already does;
- pins: run-lane deinit-order programs (the Air's `f2_min`, `d8_matchtime`, and the enum half of `e9_matchconsume`, matching Stage 0), plus a MIR golden.

The `Optional` half of `e9` is a Stage 0 bug, SL:hazards C10. Its run-lane program is marked `differs`, citing C10.

Scope note for the spec sentence: the arm-entry rule applies only to a CONSUMED scrutinee (an owned local or a by-value parameter). A `match` on a field, a tuple element, a `&`/`&var` binding or a receiver borrows it ("Match consumes an owned enum"), so a `_` there destroys nothing: it is a presence test, and the owner still ends the value. Verified on both compilers: matching `h.slot` twice prints both arms, the payload drops once at `h`'s scope end, and `move a` in an arm is refused (`transfer.move-from-borrow`). The spec sentence states the restriction, and a borrowed-scrutinee pin with a `_` arm goes beside the deinit-order pins.
