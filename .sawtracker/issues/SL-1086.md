## Description

**The borrowck lane's corpus check asks sawc for 6.3 GB of text to judge 56 refusals.** Found while reading SL-1079's server failure (Nov 12).

**Measured** (`.build/scratch/p1079/probe_records.py` in the lead's checkout): `check_corpus` in `compiler/tests/borrowck/borrowck_lane.py` runs ONE call, `sawc borrowck --dump --std-root std @LIST` over the 2,647 corpus programs, and reads its whole output as text: 6,309,493,487 bytes. Each program's record carries the dump of every std module it loads, and the lane then throws those away (`stripped_records`, `without_std_modules`). Under the fixpoint lane's twin the same call is made by both stages and compared, so one gate run holds that output several times over in memory.

**What it costs.**
- Memory: the lane holds the 6.3 GB as bytes and as text; under the twin add two captures and the hand-on. Twenty gigabytes and more at the peak, on a machine also running the other steps.
- Time: the step is about six minutes of every gate run, twice (the direct run and the replay).
- Fragility: it is what turned a 2 GiB write limit into 43 false failures (SL-1079). Any reader or writer in the path with a 32-bit length is exposed.

**What the check needs** is each program's own modules' dump: the `ERROR` lines and rule labels, and the statement labels `count_labels` counts.

**Options for the implementer to weigh, with numbers before choosing:**
- **(a) `sawc borrowck --dump` gains a way to leave std's modules out** (a flag, or the existing dump printing only the modules of the files named). The lane's strip then goes. Check what else reads the dump and relies on std's part: the golden cases (`holds_entry_module` says a strip must keep the entry module), the conformance programs, the fixpoint lane's comparison (which would then compare less: is std's dump compared anywhere else? it should be, once, not 2,647 times).
- **(b) the lane reads the output as a stream**, a record at a time, never holding more than one. Fixes memory, not time, and not the twin's copies.
- (a) and (b) together.

**Step 0:** measure where the six minutes go (sawc producing the text, the pipe, the lane's decode and strip) and what the output is with std left out (strip it and count), so the saving is known before anything is designed. Enumerate every caller of `borrowck --dump` and of `stripped_records`/`records` in the tests.

**The class (obligation 4):** every other lane call whose output is read whole and scales with corpus × std: the drops, MIR, mono and coro dumps over the corpus (`compiler/tests/*/…_lane.py`: search for `@` list calls with `--dump`). Measure each one's output size in the same step 0 and report the table; fix the ones over, say, 500 MB by the same means.

**Expected outcomes.** The same counts in every lane's summary. A dump of a few hundred megabytes or less. The borrowck step's time down by whatever step 0 shows the text was costing.

**Gate.** `compiler/tests/run.py --only seed,units,golden,subset,fixture_whitespace,std,borrowck,drops,mir,mono,coro,comments`; the fixpoint lane (the server's); `tools/battery.sh citations`.
