## Description

**`Vector.sort` and `Vector.sort_by` are insertion sorts, quadratic on unsorted input.** Found by SL-970 unit 4, which had to give the coroutine passes their own merge sort (`coro_sort`) to stay near-linear.

**Where:** `std/vector.saw` `sort` (for `T: Comparable`) and `sort_by` (with a comparator) each swap adjacent elements until the new one is in place: O(n²) comparisons and swaps, and O(n) only on already-sorted input. Both promise a stable sort, and the doc says nothing of its cost.

**Expected:** an O(n log n) stable sort, which is what a user reaching for `sort` expects ("APIs do the expected thing"). Options include a merge sort with an O(n) buffer, or a hybrid (insertion sort under a small threshold, merging above it).

- **Allocation:** the buffer is an allocation, so check design 234's rule. `sort` is an allocating op only if it allocates, and the in-place option, a stable in-place merge or block sort, avoids the change of signature.
- **Order:** stability and the ordering of equal elements must be unchanged, and no caller may see a different order. Every corpus and run-lane program that sorts must give identical output.
- **Pin:** a complexity check over a large reverse-sorted and a random vector, in the complexity lane or the std lane.

**Then:** replace `coro_sort` (`compiler/coro/src/`) and any other hand-written sort in the compiler with std's.
