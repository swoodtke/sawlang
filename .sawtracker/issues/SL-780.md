## Description

**Precise wakes for the `threads: N` pool, so an idle worker parks at no CPU cost (the spec's sentence) instead of polling every 50 ms.**

A17a (SL-578.p1) caps every reactor poll at `POOL_POLL_CAP_MILLIS` = 50 while a pool is live (`std/taskgroup.saw`, `idle_poll`, entry points `idle`, `wait_until`, `pool_wait`). Stage 0 has the same cap (`MT_POLL_CAP_NANOS`). The cap covers three missed-wake paths:
1. **A `cancel_addr` write pokes nothing.** Without the cap it hangs 2/2 runs (`.build/scratch/cap_cancel_addr.saw` in p578pool's report).
2. **An fd event can land on another thread's poll,** which latches the word and returns while the thread that schedules that frame stays blocked. Without the cap it hangs 17/30 runs (`cap_stolen_event.saw`).
3. **A poke reaches only pollers already blocked,** so a waiter between its last look and its poll can miss one. No repro was built.

**Cost, measured by the Air (SL-578.p1 review):** `getrusage` user+sys, median of 5, for a group whose one task sleeps 2 s, less a plain 2 s sleep:
- sawc2: +2.2 ms at 4 workers (~0.1% of a core), +9.4 ms at 16 workers (~0.5%);
- Stage 0: +0.6 ms at 4 workers.

That is about 0.3 ms per idle worker per second: every idle worker wakes about 20 times a second, which keeps a laptop's cores out of deep idle.

**Done when:**
- each path wakes its waiter precisely (a cancel-word write that pokes, an event routed to the thread that schedules its frame, a poke that can't be missed between look and poll);
- the cap is removed;
- the two repros pass without it, 100× per level;
- an idle 16-worker pool measures within noise of a plain sleep.

The spec's "an idle worker parks on a condition variable … at no CPU cost" (Multi-threaded execution) is the target this issue restores. Until it lands, idle pools pay bounded polling.
