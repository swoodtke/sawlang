## Description

FOLLOW-UP (b) of design 275 U3 (RULED (c) of the contract's Fences paragraph). `Vector.with_ref` / `with_var_ref` BODIES and `borrows` accessor `lend` WINDOWS across a SUSPENSION — the same argument U3 made for the `for` head, at the two spellings it deliberately left alone.

THE FENCE AS IT STANDS. LANGUAGE_SPEC's Places section says a `borrows` body is `sync` and an accessor's `lend` window may not span a suspension, and that a `with_ref`/`with_var_ref` body may not either. U3 rewrote the neighbouring sentence in *Suspension and the coroutine transform* NARROWLY: the tracked `for` window IS allowed to span one, and those two spellings RETAIN the restriction — "the same argument would lift it, but it wants a consumer sweep and rows of its own", which is this brief.

THE ARGUMENT U3 MADE, and which of its three legs each spelling has. A window may hold across a park because (1) its ORIGIN is recorded, (2) its EXTENT is a statement the checker can see, and (3) the Law of Exclusivity sees every competing safe writer, so a `&var` extent live at the head or started inside the body is the writer-beside-reader error, a `&` extent composes, a `threads: N` group refuses the frame on `Send` first, and nothing else in this task runs while the frame is parked.

- A `with_ref` / `with_var_ref` BODY has (1) and (3) — the closure's borrow charges the container's root exactly as the `for` head's does — and its (2) is the CLOSURE's extent rather than a statement's. That is the question: a closure is reached through a function value, so its body is not this frame's state machine, which is the same wall design 216's `map`/`each`/`fold` bodies hit (a suspension there is refused AT the closure). Lifting this fence therefore means answering how a non-escaping closure passed directly to a call can BE part of the caller's frame — which is a real design question and not a rule change.
- An accessor `lend` WINDOW has all three, and its shape is different in a way that matters: design 141's `lend` is a SUSPENSION OF THE ACCESSOR, not a return. The accessor stops with its frame alive, the caller's window code runs, and it RESUMES for an epilogue at the close. So an accessor window that spans a task suspension has TWO frames live at once, and the release order U3's part (i) fixes for a borrowing struct (destroy the resource, THEN end the charge) becomes "resume the accessor's epilogue, THEN end the charge" — which is exactly the CLIENT-PROVIDED cleanup operation B3 left general for.

WHAT THIS BRIEF OWES:

1. A SECOND CONSUMER SWEEP (obligation 2). U3's counted 221 `for` heads over `iter()`/`enumerated()` across examples, blade, libs, devtools, sawc/std and the read-only sawtracker sources. This one counts `with_ref`/`with_var_ref` call sites and `borrows` accessor declarations, and says for each whether a body that suspends is expressible, refused, or would change outcome.
2. ITS OWN CONFORMANCE ROWS, on U3's four axes: the accessor × suspension-position × context matrix, the root axis, the conflict axis (what a window holds and what composes beside it), and the release axis — with the accessor's EPILOGUE as the release operation rather than a drop, which is the cell U3 could not write.
3. THE `sync` FENCE'S REPLACEMENT, stated as a rule rather than removed. Today `sync` is what makes the window's extent provable; whatever replaces it has to be checkable at the declaration, because an accessor is compiled once and used from both sync and driven bodies.

NOT THIS BRIEF: the generic scoped-borrow STATEMENT (follow-up (a)), which is the non-closure spelling of `with_ref` and may make half of this moot by giving the long window a statement extent; and the exclusive-window derivation (follow-up (c)), which `with_var_ref` needs and `with_ref` does not.
