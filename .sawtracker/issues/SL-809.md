## Description

**A join inversion hangs instead of panicking when a sibling is live** (found by p807, SL-807's sweep). A task joins its own inner group's task (`h.join()`) while `main` joins that task.
- **Alone** (`n1_nested_join_inversion`): it panics cleanly, `Task.join: the task cannot finish while this join waits for it`.
- **With a live sibling ticking** (`s8a_nested_no_cancel`, `s8b_nested_sleep_cancel`): it hangs past 20 s, at -O0 and -O2. The stack shows `main`'s join resumed INSIDE the subject's nested sweep, so the outer join is driven by the inner drive and the inversion check never fires.

**The doctrine it breaks:** never hide errors. A hang is a silent failure where a clean panic exists.

**Owed:**
- the mechanism: why a nested sweep resumes an outer join's driver, and whether the inversion check needs the drive stack rather than the immediate waiter;
- the obligation-4 sweep: every join route under a nested drive (`Task.join`, a group handle's join, a pool join, a `Thread.join`);
- the fix, and pins: the two probes (in p807's worktree at `.build/scratch/p807/`) panic cleanly at both levels.

Note: SL-808 is rewriting the executor's cross-thread walks and SL-807 the park classifier. Sequence this after both.
