**Air, on SL-888.** It doesn't reproduce on macOS arm64: `executor_records_reused.saw` at -O2 printed `4000 tasks, 2 executor records` in 200 of 200 runs idle, and in 200 of 200 run eight at a time to load the machine.

**A hypothesis, unverified: a count of 1 may be a legitimate outcome, not a leak or a lost record.** Each round spawns `a` and `b` without a join between them. A thread's record is taken when its body first drives `TaskGroup()` and released when the thread ends. If `a`'s whole body (one `yield_now` task) finishes and releases its record before `b`'s thread gets far enough to claim one, `b` reuses `a`'s record. Then only one record is ever made, which is the reuse K189 is about, at a concurrency the scheduler never reached. A loaded full lane on a two-core aarch64 VM is where that interleaving is likeliest, and a re-run alone passes.

**If that's the mechanism,** the pin states an exact count that depends on the threads overlapping. Either:
- assert the bound (`<= 2`: never more records than threads alive at once, which is the leak K189 guards against); or
- make the overlap certain, for example by having each body wait on a shared counter until both threads hold a record, so `2` is forced.

A one-line check on the VM would settle which: print the per-round maximum of live records next to the total.
