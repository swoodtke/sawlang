**Downcasting an erased box, `b.is<T>()` and `b.take<T>()`, is refused as `slice.not-yet`** ("downcasting an erased box with `is` or `take` is not in the bootstrap slice yet"). After SL-721 it is the first refusal of 7 corpus programs: `erased_downcast_deinit_once`, `erased_downcast_error_retry`, `erased_downcast_generic`, `erased_downcast_is_take`, `erased_downcast_nonconforming_error`, `erased_downcast_use_after_take_error`, `erased_error_spawned_container_result` (gmgate's blocked twin).

**What SL-721 leaves in place:** the vtable header's `type_id` word is the concrete type's interned type (`compiler/llvm/src/erased.saw`, `vtable`), one per type in a program, so `is<T>()` is a compare of that word against `T`'s; a box's payload address is the fat pointer's first word, its size and alignment the vtable's, and its allocator's `dealloc` is mono's `erased_dealloc` item.

**The spec** (`any Trait` existentials, "Downcasting (v1)"): `is<T>()` borrows; `take<T>()` consumes the box either way, moving the payload out on a match (freeing the shell without its deinit) and dropping it on a miss; `T` explicit, concrete and conforming.

**Pins:** the corpus programs above, a run program per outcome (hit, miss, the payload's deinit once on each path), the nonconforming and use-after-take refusals, and the corpusdiff baseline; gmgate's twin moves when it builds.
