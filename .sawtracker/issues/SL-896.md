Found by SL-885's triage (p885); probe `variants/cancel_probe.saw` in its scratchpad. With `output()` on `/bin/sleep 1`, the canceller ran at 0 ms, yet `output()` returned `Ok(exit 0)` after 1001 ms with `cancelled() = true`. The cancel was silently dropped, against spec 'a cancel ends the WAIT with `Interrupted`'. **Mechanism (from code reading, p885):** `process_drained` (std/process.saw) never checks `cancelled()`, and its offloaded `__saw_rt_proc_read_stdout` parks until the pipe reaches EOF, which happens at the child's exit. The exit-wait loop's `cancelled()` check runs only while the child is still running, so it never fires. (`wait()` returning after the child is its documented thread-blocking behaviour, not this defect.) **Pinned by** `tests/corpus/process_cancel_during_child.saw` (wrong, by its own directives, after SL-885). **Obligation 4:** every std op that parks on an offloaded or reactor wait (output's drain, read, accept, connect, a channel receive, sleep) checked for a cancel landing mid-wait.

**Step 1 (p896):** SL-896 reproduces, and its mechanism is confirmed. The drain is an offloaded `__saw_rt_proc_read_stdout`, which by spec isn't a cancellation point, and the exit wait's `cancelled()` check only runs while the child is alive. The sweep: `output()`'s drain is the ONLY dropped cell. TCP read, write, accept and connect, `Channel.receive`, `sleep` and `SignalWatch.next` all honour a cancel within ~1 ms.
- **RULED (lead): the seam design.** `__saw_rt_proc_take_stdout(job)` hands the pipe's read end to std, which drains it non-blocking through `io_wait` (the one cancellable wait), so a cancel ends it with `Interrupted`. On cancel the pipe is closed, the child is not killed, and it is reaped by the orphan sweep.
