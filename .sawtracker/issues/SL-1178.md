**`suite_lock.sh acquire --yield-to-gate` can starve a local waiter indefinitely.**

**What happened (Nov 24):** an agent's `acquire --yield-to-gate` waited about 4.4 hours.
- The waiter yields to every server gate run that is running or queued.
- When patches arrive steadily (that day: SL-1170.p1, SL-1156.p7 twice, SL-1012.p2 and p3, SL-1159.p2 and SL-1161.p1), a gate run is almost always queued, so the waiter never takes the lock.
- When the lock briefly went free, another local waiter took it first.
- Other agents in the same session lost from 15 minutes to hours the same way. One killed its waiter and skipped its gate.

**The rule it should keep:** yielding protects the server's gate from a local suite stealing its slot. The yield needs a bound, so a local waiter still makes progress.

**Options** (the fix chooses and records why):
- **(a) A yield budget:** after a waiter has yielded for N minutes in total (for example 45), it stops yielding and queues FIFO with the gate.
- **(b) A turn:** each local waiter, once it has been passed over by K gate runs, takes the next free slot before a newly queued gate.
- **(c) A FIFO ticket queue** shared by local waiters and the server's gate. The gate keeps priority only over waiters that arrived after it.

**Test:** `tools/test_suite_lock*` (the `suitelock` battery lane), with a stub tracker that always reports a queued gate. The waiter must take the lock within the bound.
