**Profile sawc2 Stage 2 compiling itself: where does the time go?** (The user, Oct 12: launch a profiling pass, and give it to the Air, since its machine is quieter and so its results are more consistent.)

**Why.** After SL-528 (shared failure helpers; checked accessors now inline), Stage 2 equals Stage 1 on `typecheck --check` (0.36s) but is still about 1.2× Stage 1 on `build --emit-ll -O2` (5.54–5.85s against 4.65–4.88s, measured on the busy Studio). Stage 1 is the Stage 0-built sawc2 of the same tree, so the gap is purely codegen quality: sawc2's against Stage 0's, for the same source. Doctrine: perf by measurement. This issue is the measurement; fixes are filed from it.

**The tree.** SL-528.p1's tested commit (`patch show SL-528.p1`) or main once it merges. Without SL-528, the top of the profile is the bug already fixed.

**Method** (on the Air):
1. Build Stage 1 and Stage 2 (`compiler/tools/build.py`). Note the machine, its load and the clang version.
2. **The jobs:** `build --emit-ll -O2` and `typecheck --check` of `compiler/driver/src/main.saw` with `--std-root std` and every stage's `--module-path`. `.build/scratch/bench/selfjob.py` on the Studio lists the exact arguments; reproduce it.
3. **Wall time,** best of 5, for each binary and job.
4. **A sampling profile of both binaries on `build --emit-ll`:** `xctrace record --template 'Time Profiler'`, or `sample <pid>`, or anything comparable that gives per-function self and total time with symbols. sawc2's functions carry readable `saw <module>.<function>` names.

   - the top 25 functions by self time for each binary, side by side;

**SL-591 profile (Air).** The headline: one missing attribute explains the whole gap. Marking `Vector<Int>.push`'s `&var self` as `noalias` takes Stage 2 from 1.045× Stage 1 to **0.982×**, faster than Stage 1, with byte-identical output. The details follow, then the issues filed, ranked.

**Setup.**
- **Machine:** MacBook Air, Apple M5, 10 cores, 24 GB, load average 1.5–2.2 throughout; Apple clang 21.0.0.
- **The tree:** SL-528.p1 applied to cf75ca1e (tree ee353597, the patch's exact tree), with Stage 1 and Stage 2 built by `compiler/tools/build.py`.
- **The jobs:** the lead's `selfjob.py` with the paths pointed at my checkout, so `--std-root std` and the 12 stage `--module-path`s on `compiler/driver/src/main.saw`.
- **Scripts:** everything is under `.build/scratch/air-review/sl591/` on the Air.

**1. Wall time.**

| job | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `typecheck --check`, best of 5 | 0.34 s | 0.33 s | equal |
| `build --emit-ll -O2`, best of 5 | 4.26 s | 4.44 s | 1.04 |
| `build --emit-ll -O2`, 10 interleaved runs (`spread.py`), min / median / max | 4.254 / 4.272 / 4.306 s | 4.449 / 4.516 / 4.654 s | median 1.057 |

Both builds produce the same 28,879,373 bytes. **On the Air the gap is about 5 %, not the Studio's 1.2×.** The Studio's figure was most likely inflated by load. The ranges don't overlap, so the 5 % is real.

**2. Sampling profile.** macOS `sample` at 1 ms, 3 runs per binary, self samples pooled (`profile.py`). Names are re-keyed to (package, type, function) across the two manglings (`analyze2.py`).

- **Functions whose increase exceeds 10 % of the gap (120 samples):**

**3. Mechanisms.**
- **(M1) No `noalias`: it is THE gap, by experiment.** sawc2's `.ll` has zero `noalias` (and no TBAA) in 28 MB. Stage 0 marks `self` `noalias`: compare `define internal %"Result$2$Void$AllocError" @"Vector$2$Int$GlobalAllocator_push"(%Vector* noalias %self, i64 %value)` with sawc2's `define internal void @"…Vector.push…"(ptr %ret, ptr %p1, i64 %p2)`.
  - **In the machine code** (`fastpath.py`): after `buf[self.length] = move value`, Stage 2 must assume the store may alias `self`. So it RELOADS `self.length` and repeats the overflow check (`ldr x8,[x20,#0x10]; adds; b.vs`), where Stage 1 stores the `length + 1` it already computed. That dependent load sits on the loop-carried chain of every run of pushes to one vector.
  - **The experiment** (`add_noalias.py`, `spread3.py`): Stage 1's emitted `.ll` of the driver, with ONLY `push`'s `%p1` marked `noalias` (sound: `&var self` is exclusive), and the same `.ll` unchanged as the baseline, both built by clang -O2 against the same runtime. 10 interleaved runs each:
    - Stage 1: median 4.261 s;
    - Stage 2 baseline: 4.453 s (1.045);
    - Stage 2 + `noalias` on `push`: **4.183 s (0.982)**.

    Output is identical (sha256 `92ec61a6…`). `push` is still called out of line at all 821 sites; the fast path goes from 28 instructions to 27, with no load after the store.
- **(M2) Aggregate results go through an out-pointer, even for `internal` functions.** Stage 0 returns `Result<Void, AllocError>` by value, in registers (`mov w0, wzr` / caller `cbnz w0`). sawc2 passes `ptr %ret` (not even marked `sret`): the callee stores the tag (`str wzr,[x19]`), the caller reloads it (`ldr w8,[sp]`), and every argument moves up a register. That's real per-call work on every fallible call, but I haven't isolated its share: rewriting 821 call sites by hand isn't worth it, and with M1 alone Stage 2 is already faster than Stage 1.
- **Measured as NOT a mechanism: `copy_bytes`.** The new std's `copy_bytes` is a Saw loop (`std/mem.saw`) where Stage 0's std calls `memcpy`. clang -O2 turns it into an 8-byte-per-iteration loop inlined into `push`'s growth path. A Stage 2 rebuilt with `copy_bytes` calling libc `memcpy` (`memcpy_std.py`, `exp_memcpy/`) is no faster: median 4.505 s against 4.461 s, identical output. Its profile moves only about 140 samples out of `push` into `memmove`. No issue filed.
- **Measured, not explained: `free` + `bzero`, +344.** In the frame-pointer build's call graph (below), the increase is spread proportionally, about +25 %, over the SAME callers in both binaries: `push`'s growth, `init_walk_block`, `init_analysis`, `StringBuilder.append`. That points to more or larger frees in the same places, not a new allocation site, and I found no single mechanism. No issue filed; a second look after M1 lands is cheap.
- **(M3) Found on the way: sawc2-built binaries have no frame pointers.** sawc2's `.ll` carries no `"frame-pointer"` attribute, so clang omits them: 14 functions set up `x29` in Stage 2, against 1,142 in Stage 1. `sample` then can't unwind: 86 % of Stage 2's `push` samples had NO caller. The caller attribution above needed a rebuilt Stage 2 (`add_fp.py`: `"frame-pointer"="non-leaf"` on every `define`, 1,444 frames). Apple's arm64 convention expects x29 to address a valid frame record, and debuggers and crash reports rely on it. Not a speed item.

**Issues filed, ranked by share of the gap:**
1. **SL-592 (M1):** emit `noalias` on `&var` parameters. It closes the whole gap and more (0.982× Stage 1).
2. **SL-593 (M2):** return aggregates by value for internal functions. Real per-call work; share not isolated.
3. **SL-594 (M3):** emit frame pointers. Diagnosability, not speed.

Not filed: `copy_bytes` (measured as no effect) and `free`/`bzero` (proportional, no single mechanism).
