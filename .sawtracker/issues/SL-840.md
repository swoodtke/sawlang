## Description

**Two-phase receiver borrows: an argument may read the call's own receiver** (user ruling, Oct 23, SL-425 c26; SL:hazards S4).

**The rule:** in `recv.m(args)`, where `m` takes `&var self`, the receiver's exclusive borrow begins after the arguments are evaluated. So an argument may READ the receiver, provided no reference from that read survives into the call: `c.write(c.read())` and `v.push(v.len())` are legal, while `sink(&var p.x, reset(&var p))` and `p.total(reset(&var p))` stay refused (a reference into the same root lives through the call). sawc2's borrowck already accepts the two-phase form (`golden/calls.saw`); this unit makes the spec and the subset checker agree.

**Scope:**
- **Spec:** the "Nested calls" paragraph is rewritten to the rule, with the examples above, and the receiver sentence ("The receiver is borrowed for the whole call expression") is replaced.
- **Conformance:** a row for "an argument may read its call's receiver; a reference into the root may not span the call". Written first.
- **Pins:** borrowck fixtures for each legal and refused shape; check `golden/calls.saw`.
- **Retire the `root-reuse` rule** (`compiler/tools/subset_check.py`) and re-canonicalize its markers: 118 in `compiler/`, plus 2 in `std/task.saw` from SL-425 U3. Every restore is verified by the compiler building itself (Stage 1 and 2) and the gate; the std ones by their exercising programs at -O0, -O2 and ASan.

After SL-842 (the intersection lift), which retires the Stage 0 acceptance rules, so this doesn't collide with it.
