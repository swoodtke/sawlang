**Found by SL-591's profile (Air). Not a speed item; it breaks profiling, debugging and crash backtraces of every sawc2-built program.**

**The mechanism.** sawc2's `.ll` carries no `"frame-pointer"` function attribute, so clang -O2 omits frame pointers:

| binary | functions that set up `x29` |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 (Stage 0 through llvmlite) | 1,142 |
| Stage 2 | **14** |

Apple's arm64 convention expects x29 to always address a valid frame record, and profilers, lldb and crash reports walk that chain.

**The consequence, measured:** with `sample` on Stage 2's self-compile, **86 %** of `Vector.push`'s samples had NO caller (the stack ends at `push`). SL-591 needed a Stage 2 rebuilt with `"frame-pointer"="non-leaf"` on every `define` (1,444 frames) to get a call graph at all. sawc2's own panic backtraces depend on the backtrace table, not the frame chain, but every external tool depends on the chain.

**Fix.** Emit `"frame-pointer"="non-leaf"` on every function on Darwin, as clang does for C there; `"all"` is also defensible. Decide Linux and freestanding explicitly: Linux has no such convention, but profiling still benefits. Measure the cost; it's usually around 1 %.

**Done when:**
- sawc2's `.ll` carries the attribute, with a golden pin;
- `sample` of Stage 2 on the self-compile attributes callers, with no `<root>` majority for hot leaves;
- the self-compile time is reported before and after.
