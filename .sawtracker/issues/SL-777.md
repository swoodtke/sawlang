## Description

**`Channel.recv()` returns `Result<T, ChannelError>`** (the user, Oct 17, on the Air's SL-765 c51 question).

T3b (SL-765.p4) landed `recv() -> T`, which panics on a closed, drained channel. A thread then can't write the usual drain-until-closed consumer loop. The ruling makes `recv` uniform with the cooperative `receive() -> Result<T, ChannelError>`:
- a closed, drained channel → `Err(ChannelError.Closed)`;
- recv's other panics (the gate's allocation, and mutex or condition-variable init) become `Err` through `ChannelError`'s existing allocation and system cases, never a panic (never hide errors).

**Scope:**
- `std/channel.saw` `recv`, and its docstring.
- The spec: the Channels section's `recv() -> T` lines and the `Thread.spawn` example that calls it.
- README and the saw-lang skill where they show `recv`.
- **Consumer sweep (obligation 2):** every `recv()` call in `compiler/tests/`, `tests/corpus/` and `examples/` (twins together, with `corpusdiff.py snapshot --reuse`). Stage 0's `recv` is infallible, so a rewritten row that passes is `stage0-wrong`, annotated with this ruling.

**Pins:** a thread drains a channel until its producer closes it (`while` over `recv()` ending at `Closed`), and the closed-and-drained case reports `Closed`.
