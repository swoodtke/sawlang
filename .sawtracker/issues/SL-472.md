The rule is coherent with SL-469's ruling: an escaping closure may neither consume nor mutate a capture, and `Arc<Mutex<T>>` is the one way to carry changing state. One trap for the brief. The spec lowers some source constructs INTO non-escaping closures: "the closure a place window is lowered into" is how `try! slots[0].push(move h)` consumes `h` once (LANGUAGE_SPEC, the `move` capture into a non-escaping closure). In those bodies the compiler, not the user, chose what is captured. If `place_verdict` treats a lowered window body's environment as user by-value captures, it will refuse legitimate writes the window makes through its lent place, or the transfer of a `move` capture. So pin as ACCEPTED rows beside the refusal matrix: (1) `slots[0].push(move h)` through a place window; (2) a `borrows` accessor's window body writing its lent place; (3) a `with_var_ref`-style non-escaping closure writing through its `&var` parameter; (4) a non-escaping `[move v]` closure doing `var w = move v; w.push(1)` (the idiom the spec will name). # Typecheck batch A: the closure rulings (SL-472 "captures are let", SL-469 "an escaping closure may not consume a capture")

- **Epic:** SL-398. These are two user rulings (Oct 9), both enforced in sawc2's typecheck. They run concurrently with U6d2 (SL-460, the borrow checker's loans), which works in `compiler/borrowck/` and `compiler/mir/`.
- **Read first:** SL-472's comments c1–c4 and SL-469's comments c1–c4 (all of them; the matrix rows are there), LANGUAGE_SPEC's closure section (design 132's "Assigning to a by-value capture is a compile error" bullet, around the `run_owned` example), and SL-462's `place_verdict` funnel (`compiler/typecheck/src/mutability.saw`) with the typecheck README's "Safety readings".

## The rulings (quoted; agents can't post kind=ruling)
- **SL-472** (the user): "For a non escaping closure capturing the vec by ref would be the correct behavior imo. For an escaping closure no captures should be modifiable ever." Then: "Confirmed. Mutex is modifiable and pointers are unsafe and modifiable, both by design."
  - **The lead's reading, one rule:** a by-value, `move` or `copy` capture is a `let` inside the closure body, in every closure. The only way a closure changes outside state is a reference capture `[&var x]`, which only a non-escaping closure may take. This replaces design 132's lost-write rule and its Vector carve-out.
- **SL-469** (the user): "Compile error". A consuming use of a `move` capture inside an ESCAPING closure body is refused at compile time. Non-escaping closures keep the existing take-once rule, with no runtime once-flag.

## Decisions (the lead)
1. **SL-472, the funnel.** A by-value/move/copy capture is an immutable root in `place_verdict`, so every write shape is refused by the one funnel with `mutability.immutable` (or a capture-specific sub-id under it, your call): `x = v`, `x += v`, `x.f = v`, `v[i] = y`, a `&var self` call, `&var x`, `swap`/`take()`. Delete design 132's separate check; leave no second copy.
   - **The funnel's docstring states which captures are judged:** user-declared and implicit captures, not a lowering's synthesized environment (a place window's closure).
   - **Allowed rows** (each an accepted fixture or golden row):
     - `&self` interior-mutability methods on a captured value (`Mutex.lock`, `Atomic` ops);
     - writes to a captured `UnsafePointer`'s pointee (unsafe domain);
     - an implicit `self` capture follows the receiver: a non-escaping closure in a `&var self` method writing `self.n += 1` is accepted (in a `&self` method it's refused, as today);
     - writing through a captured reference: a plain capture of a `&var` parameter `r`, then `r.x = 1`, in a non-escaping closure;
     - a nested non-escaping `[&var x]` of an outer `[&var x]` (a reborrow) is accepted; of an outer by-value capture it's refused;
     - the compiler-lowered window closures (the Air, SL-472 c2): `slots[0].push(move h)` through a place window; a `borrows` accessor's window body writing its lent place; a `with_var_ref`-style non-escaping closure writing through its `&var` parameter;
     - the non-escaping `[move v]` idiom `var w = move v; w.push(1)`.
   - **The headline refusal:** the 2-3-4 program (an escaping `[move v]` closure writing `v[0]`, copied with `let g = f`) is refused; the same program with a non-escaping `[&var v]` is accepted.
2. **SL-469, a typecheck rule** with an id beside `capture.*` (say `capture.escaping-consume`). Refuse a consuming use of a `move` capture in an escaping closure body.
