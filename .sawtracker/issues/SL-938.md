## Description

**Port `std.cbor` to the new std: `CborEncoder: Encoder` and `CborDecoder: Decoder`.** Stage 0's `sawc/std/cbor.saw` is 1,113 lines. The new std has the wire contract (`std/CBOR.md`) and the vectors (`tests/cbor_vectors/accept`, `reject`), but no module. Part of SL-596.

**Depends on SL-833** (the serde traits `Encoder`/`Decoder`/`Serialize`/`Deserialize`, and `EncodeError`/`DecodeError`). Land it after.

**Order (user, Oct 30):** cbor before Blade (SL-936). Blade's `lock.saw` CBOR half and `tests/lock_cbor.saw` need it, and that test becomes one of this port's pins.

**Shape:** a straight port to the current language and std, with the vectors as the oracle: every accept vector round-trips byte-exact, and every reject vector is refused with its stated fault. Expected tier: Sonnet, under a no-guessing rule (an undetermined behaviour is flagged OPEN, never invented).

**Corpus rows it should move:** the `cbor169_*` programs' twins in `tests/corpus/`, `d249_both_std_encoders_bare_is_a_tie_error` and `d249_std_json_and_cbor_both_name_encode` (the last two named on SL-833).

Approved patch SL-938.p1 (revision 1)

**Air: SL-938.p1 approved.** Reviewed r1 on e0f7d5294. Your three probes cover the encoder's counting rules, its limits and allocation refusals, and the decoder's typed reads. Mine are about the decoder over untrusted bytes.

**Hardening** (`sl938p/h1_hardening.saw`): ASan at -O0 and -O2, identical output, clean. The base input is a 73-byte blob of a derived `Rec`. It holds `Int64.max`, a negative `Int`, a `Bool`, an absent `String?`, a nested struct with a `Vector<String>` (including 2-, 3- and 4-byte UTF-8), a `Vector<Int>` up to `Int.min`, and `UInt64.max`.
- **Every proper prefix (73)** is `Truncated`.
- **Every single-byte mutation:** each position × 13 replacements (00 FF 1F 5F 7F 9F BF F7 F9 C0 1B, the byte xor 0x80 and the byte + 1), 949 inputs. `open` accepts 536, and **every accepted one transcodes back to the identical bytes**, which is the profile's "the bytes are the value". 405 are rejected across Truncated, Malformed, TooManyItems, NotCanonical, Unsupported and TrailingBytes. 522 still decode as `Rec`, and the rest are an `Err`, never a panic.
- **200,000 random inputs of 1–12 bytes:** 4,951 accepted, all canonical, and none rejected by a panic.
- **Accepted but untranscodable:** 8 of the mutations and 31 of the random inputs. Every one is a major-1 item whose argument is past `Int64.max`: valid CBOR that Saw can't hold, reported as `OutOfRange` at that item.

**Edge table** (`sl938p/e1_edges.saw`): ASan, 67 rows, each giving the fault std/CBOR.md names at the expected offset.
- **Shortest form** on both sides of every width boundary (23/24, 255/256, 65535/65536, 2^32−1/2^32), for a text length, an array count and a negative too.
- **Malformed and unsupported heads:** reserved info 28–30 are `Malformed`. Indefinite bytes, text, array and map, and a lone break, are `Unsupported`.
- **Map keys:** out of order is `NotCanonical` at the key, and a duplicate is `Malformed`, container keys included. The order is bytewise (`"a"` before `"aa"`, `100` before `"a"`).
- **Floats, tags and simple values:** float16, float32, float64, tags (the uint64-argument form too), `undefined`, simple 0 and a 1-byte simple are all `Unsupported`.
- **UTF-8:** overlong C0/E0, surrogate ED A0, past U+10FFFF, an F5 lead, a lone continuation, and a sequence cut by the payload end are all `Malformed`. Byte strings aren't checked.
- **One top-level item:** trailing bytes and empty input.
- **Length claims of 2^63 and 2^64−1:** `Truncated` for bytes and text, `TooManyItems` for an array or map, with no allocation.
- **`max_items` at its edge,** counting containers and keys.
- **Depth:** 64 deep at 64 is accepted, 65 deep is `TooDeep` at byte 64, and an empty innermost container is free. That matches the encoder's rule: at `max_depth: 0` a scalar or `[]` passes and `[1]` doesn't. 10,000 deep is refused at byte 64 by the work stack, with no recursion.

**Reading the code:** `finish()` hands out `out.copy()`, a refcount retain that allocates nothing, so the "Alloc, not a panic" promise holds there. The positional reads and `skip()` lean on `open`'s whole-input validation, unlike JSON's decoder, and the hardening run shows that's sound. The spec's `std.cbor` API names match the public surface (`open(bytes:max_depth:max_size:max_items:)`, `finish`, `rewind`, `transcode(to:)`, `encode`).

**Gates** (full logs, from `exit=` lines):
