**sawos's request (m1733, Nov 26), not a blocker.** sawos wraps its kernel tables in structs with a private `Slab` field and a typed accessor:

```saw-fragment
func at(&var self, p: ProcessIndex) borrows -> &var T { lend self.slab[p] }
```

so a record index can't reach the body table. At `-Oz`, each `at` stays an out-of-line wrapper around `Slab.[]`: about 250 bytes of text on riscv32 and 280 on arm64 for two tables, growing with each table.

**The question:** whether sawc inlines a lend accessor whose body is only a re-lend of another place.

**Two paths, in order:**
1. **The compiler's own choice (the lead's to rule).** For a `borrows` accessor whose body is a single `lend` of a place reached through another accessor, sawc gives LLVM an inline hint, or `alwaysinline` if the hint isn't enough at `-Oz`. No language change. First measure why LLVM keeps the wrapper at `-Oz`, from the IR: the call cost, the bounds check, or the accessor's prologue/epilogue. Fix it from that.
2. **`@inline`.** It is reserved in the spec "for a later design". Making it available is a language decision for the owner, taken only if path 1 can't cover the shape.

**Evidence owed before dispatch:** sawos's reduced program, and a `-Oz` riscv32 and arm64 `--emit-ll` and objdump of the wrapper.

**Owner ruling (Nov 26), in the lead's session:** "i have no problem adding an @inline if we have a use for it."

**How the lead applies it:**
- `@inline` is open. It needs a use, shown by measurement.
- sawos's reduced program is the test. Measure first why `-Oz` keeps the wrapper, from the IR.
  - If a compiler-chosen hint covers this shape, it lands on its own, and `@inline` waits for a use the hint can't reach.
  - If the hint can't cover it, `@inline` is designed with this program as its pin.
- **The design's proposed shape, for the owner to confirm when it's written:**
  - `@inline` on a function or method (`alwaysinline`) and `@inline(never)` (`noinline`), with no plain hint form;
  - it is refused on an `@export` and on an `extern`;
  - it is honoured across modules and codegen units;
  - an unsatisfiable `@inline`, such as a recursive one, is a compile error, not a silent ignore.

**Lead's measurement and ruling (Nov 26).** The program is sawos's `repro.saw` (chat f573). I built it with main's sawc at `-Oz --freestanding --no-hidden-alloc` for riscv32 (`+m,+a,+c`), then optimized the emitted IR with the target's clang. The files are in the lead's checkout, `.build/scratch/sl1199/`.

**Why the wrapper stays.** After optimization, `RecordTable.at`'s prologue is:
```
%l7 = alloca [4 x i8]
%t5 = call @"saw prologue repro.Slab.[]..."(ptr %l7, ptr @RECORDS, i32 %p3)
%0 = load i32, ptr %l7
store i32 %0, ptr %p1
ret ptr %t5
```
- The inner window's state goes to a local buffer and is copied into the outer prologue's state argument (`%p1`). So the body is never a bare forwarded call.
- Even with the copy removed by hand, LLVM's `-Oz` inline threshold keeps a forwarder that makes one call.

| riscv32 `.text` (bytes) | text | `at` wrappers |
|---|---|---|
| today | 8950 | 2 |
| inner state passed in place | 8794 | 2 |
| `alwaysinline` on the two prologues | 8766 | 0 |

**Ruling: the compiler's choice covers this shape, so `@inline` is not needed for it.** The owner's OK for `@inline` (c2) stays open for a use this doesn't reach.

**The work:**
1. **A re-lend accessor is always inlined.** A `borrows` accessor whose body is a single `lend` of a place reached through another accessor (no other statement, the lend in tail position) gets `alwaysinline` on its prologue and epilogue. Inlining a forwarder never grows code.
   - Name the shape in one funnel that decides it, with its ENTRY POINTS.
   - The pin: a `--emit-ll` check that the attribute is there, and an `-Oz` object check that sawos's two wrapper symbols are gone (riscv32 and arm64), with the bare-slab control unchanged.
   - Something that fails the shape keeps today's code: a second statement, a lend with a computation around it, or a conditional lend.
2. **A prologue that nests an inner window whose state it carries across the lend** passes its own state slot in place, at the inner state's offset, rather than a local buffer then a copy. The epilogue does the same. That's the 156-byte part, and it helps every nested accessor, inlined or not.
   - Pins: the layout and coro goldens move, and those diffs are read; the run lane is unchanged.

Both run on the hosted profile too. The lead's gate is `run.py --only seed,units,golden,subset,fixture_whitespace,std,comments,coro,programs`, plus the new pins. The server runs the rest.

Approved patch SL-1199.p1 (revision 1)

**Approved.** Unit 1 of c3. I built it at 5aad52e1b and probed past the patch's own case.

**The funnel** (`ll_relend_forwarder`) is conservative. A half qualifies only if it has exactly one window call of the right kind with no absent edge, no switch, open or unreachable terminator, and only copy, reference and conversion statements. The prologue and the epilogue must both qualify. Drops, arithmetic, budget points and conditional lends keep today's code. `noinline` appears in sawc only on panic paths and SL-1190's pad function, so `alwaysinline` meets no conflicting attribute.

**Probes:**
- **A generic wrapper over an owning element.** `Table<String>.at` re-lends `Store<String>.[]`, which re-lends `Vector`'s subscript. A write and two reads through the lend are correct and clean under `--sanitize address` at `-O0`, `-O2` and `-Oz`. Both forwarders' halves (`Table.at` and `Store.[]`, prologue and epilogue) carry `alwaysinline` in the `-Oz` `.ll`.
- **A receiver-less static-root re-lend:** `count_again() borrows(sync, BOOT.count)` lends `boot_count()`. The writes land (`count=42`) at `-O0` and `-Oz`.
- **Recursion cannot meet the attribute:** two accessors re-lending each other are refused before MIR ("an accessor that forwards a lend to itself, through other accessors or directly, is not lowered to MIR"), so `alwaysinline` never sees a cycle.

**Unit 2 dropped:** the measurement you gave (+0 bytes on riscv32 and +8 on arm64 once unit 1 inlines) justifies leaving it, per the measure-first rule.
